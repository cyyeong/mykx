# How to answer: a routine for every Paper 2 component

Each routine has a short name so Kix can call it out mid-session ("Clue First!"). Teach
one routine at a time, practise it on three items, then mix it with other components.
At P6 most lost marks are method, not knowledge, so these are the fastest gains.

## 1. Grammar and vocabulary MCQ: CLUE FIRST

1. **Cover the options.** Read the sentence alone.
2. **Circle the clue**: a time phrase (*by the time, since, yesterday*), a subject to
   agree with (*neither … nor*, *the number of*), a signal word (*although, because, but*),
   a negative word for tags (*nobody, hardly*).
3. **Predict** the answer, then uncover and find it.
4. **Read each option back into the sentence** and cross out the wrong ones with a reason.

Traps: the nearest noun is not always the subject (*the box of crayons is*); in
*neither … nor* the verb agrees with the nearer subject; *nobody* takes *they* in a tag.

## 2. Vocabulary cloze: WHOLE PARAGRAPH

1. Read the **whole paragraph** before choosing anything: the clue is often a sentence
   later (*the plants began to droop* explains a dry word earlier).
2. For each blank, decide what the passage needs: a feeling, a condition, an action.
3. Test collocation: which word naturally goes with its neighbours (*draw up a roster*,
   *heavy rain*, *a parched field*)?
4. Reread the sentence with your choice. Does the story still make sense?

## 3. Visual text: PURPOSE, DETAILS, TRAPS

1. **Purpose:** read the title, the line under it and the call to action. Ask "What does
   the writer want the reader to do?"
2. **Details:** for each question, find the box or line it is about before looking at the
   options. Underline numbers, ages, prices and dates.
3. **Traps:** conditions inside one box are stricter than the poster's headline (*ages 10
   and above* inside a workshop box); "free entry" is not "everything is free"; a NOT-true
   question needs every option checked.

## 4. Grammar cloze: NAME THE GAP

1. Read the whole passage once. Note the tense of the story.
2. For each blank, **name** the kind of word it needs in pencil: relative (who / whose /
   which), connector (contrast or reason), preposition (fixed phrase such as *afraid of*),
   pronoun (matches the doer), determiner.
3. Choose from the box and **tick it off**: each word is used once, so the last blanks get
   easier.
4. Reread each sentence aloud in your head.

## 5. Editing: SOUND, TIME, BOSS

Each marked word is wrong. Check in this order and stop at the first problem:

1. **Sound** (spelling): say it slowly in syllables. Check double letters (*sug-gest*),
   *i before e except after c* (*relieved, received*), suffix rules (*happily, stopped*),
   silent letters.
2. **Time** (tense): what tense is the story in? Keep it consistent (*stayed … until they
   arrived*).
3. **Boss** (agreement and form): who is doing it, one or many (*three hundred pupils
   were*)? Is it the right word form (*advice* noun, *advise* verb)?

Write only the corrected word, spelt correctly.

## 6. Comprehension cloze: BEFORE AND AFTER

1. Read the whole passage first for the topic and tense.
2. For each blank read **the words before and after** it: a noun after the blank needs a
   preposition or determiner; a base verb after it needs a helping verb (*may bite*); a
   comma before it and a new clause after it needs a connector (*, so people should …*).
3. Try the most common word of that kind first: *to, at, in, on, of, for, by, with, and,
   but, so, because, although, which, who, it, they, the, a, was, were, had, may, can*.
4. **One word only**, spelt correctly. If a second word fits as well, the passage's
   meaning decides.

## 7. Synthesis and transformation: KEEP, CHANGE, CHECK

1. **Keep** the meaning: underline the facts in the given sentence(s): who, did what,
   when, why. All must survive.
2. **Change** only what the given word forces:
   * *Despite / In spite of* + noun or -ing phrase (*Despite the wet ground …*), never a
     full clause.
   * *No sooner had …* + *than*; *Hardly had …* + *when*.
   * Reported speech: pronouns, tense back one step (*will → would*), time and place words
     (*tomorrow → the next day, here → there*).
   * Passive: object becomes subject, *be* in the same tense + past participle.
3. **Check**: one sentence only, capital letter and full stop, no extra connector (never
   *Although … but*), every fact still there.

Each item is worth 2 marks; a wrong sentence pattern usually scores 0, so the pattern
matters more than any one word.

## 8. Comprehension OE: QUESTION, LOCATE, ANSWER IN FULL

1. **Question:** underline the question word (*why, how, which word*) and the marks.
   Two marks usually means two ideas: a point and its evidence, or a feeling and why.
2. **Locate:** find the lines the question is about before writing anything.
3. **Answer in full:** a complete sentence that repeats the question's key words. Lift the
   exact words when the question asks for evidence or a word from the passage; use your
   own words when it asks you to explain.
4. For "what does this show about the character": name a quality, then the action that
   shows it.
5. For "how did she feel": name the feeling, then copy the words that show it (*her throat
   felt tight*).

## 9. The ten-minute check

1. Booklet A: every bubble shaded once; starred questions answered.
2. Cloze and editing: one word per blank, spelt correctly, capital letter where a
   sentence starts.
3. Synthesis: one sentence, the given word in the given place, full stop.
4. Comprehension: every question answered (a best attempt beats a blank); two-mark
   answers have two ideas.
