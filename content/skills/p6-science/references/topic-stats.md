## What this means — target weights for a 12-day plan
Blend of the school-paper statistics and the most recent PSLE. Use these to bias question selection and revision time.
Frequency is not the whole story: a topic that is frequent AND commonly misunderstood earns extra time.

| Tier | Topics | Suggested share of practice | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — every paper, many marks** | Interactions in environment (food webs, populations, adaptations, man's impact); Forces & magnets; Electricity; Energy conversion; Water cycle | ~50% | Each ≈7–18 marks per paper; Booklet B loves them; misconceptions cluster here (prey/predator direction, series vs parallel, heat vs temperature, PE/KE) |
| **B — reliable 5–9 marks** | Human systems; Reproduction (plants+humans); Photosynthesis & plant transport; Heat; Matter | ~35% | Frequently paired with experiment skills; keyword-sensitive in Booklet B |
| **C — 2–4 marks, quick wins** | Classification & living things (incl. bacteria/fungi); Materials; Light & shadows; Life cycles | ~15% | Mostly MCQ, rule-based, fast to secure. Never skip: 8 cheap marks per paper |
| **Cross-cutting** | Experimental skills (variables, fair test, aim/hypothesis/conclusion, control, reliability vs accuracy, procedure) | inside every session | ≈18 marks/paper in school papers, about 11 in the PSLE. The single biggest lever in Booklet B |

## Recurring sub-skills per topic (what recent school papers actually asked)
Use these as the checklist when choosing or writing questions. Each appears in ≥2 of the 6 papers.
- **INT-ENV**: read two population graphs → deduce food chain (prey falls while predator rises); add/remove an organism and predict 2-step effects; classify producer/prey/predator/"both prey and predator"; define population/community/habitat; structural vs behavioural adaptation with **feature → what it does → how that helps survival**; camouflage; deforestation → habitat loss, soil erosion, more CO₂/global warming; fossil fuels → CO₂.
- **INT-FORCE**: identify all forces on an object (gravity always; friction if touching; magnetic if magnet + magnetic material; spring if stretched/compressed); friction direction opposite to motion; force vs ramp angle graph; springs: same spring + heavier load → longer stretch, spring force = load; stacked/compressed springs; "which is NOT an effect of a force" (light, shadows); magnets: attracted-only = magnetic material, repelled = magnet; stroking & electrical methods; iron temporary vs steel permanent; magnet strength from distance a clip is attracted; electromagnet strength (more coils/batteries).
- **SYS-ELEC**: switch/observation table → circuit; which bulb fusing switches off others; brightness ranking (bulbs in series dim, batteries in series brighten, parallel branches independent); circuit tester on hidden wiring/materials; draw/complete a circuit to meet a description; safety (insulation; don't overload).
- **ENE-CONV**: energy chains for toy car, wind turbine, dam, water wheel, heater, stirrer; GPE ↔ KE on slopes/ramps/bouncing ball with heat+sound losses; bar/line graphs of energy at a point; heavier/higher → more GPE → more KE → deeper dent / further travel; friction (rough/sand) → more heat, less KE.
- **CYC-WATER**: temperature–time graphs (flat at 0 °C melting, flat at 100 °C boiling; what changed at minute X); where droplets form (outside cold container / inside lid of warm container; none at room temperature); explain mist/white fumes (water vapour meets cooler air/surface, loses heat, condenses); why mist disappears (gains heat, evaporates); evaporation factors (surface area, wind, temperature) and how to design/shorten the experiment.
- **SYS-HUMAN**: systems working together flow chart; blood oxygen/CO₂ along lungs–heart–legs; more blood to muscles during exercise (comparative: faster, more oxygen & digested food, remove more CO₂); digestive organs and what each does; enclosed-room gas changes; breathing-rate tables.
- **CYC-REPRO**: order germination→pollination→fertilisation→dispersal; flower parts & roles; pollinator clues (wind vs insect; smell of rotting meat = flies); dispersal from a distance graph / fruit structure; why disperse; germination conditions & hypothesis wording; human parts that produce reproductive cells; traits passed on.
- **ENE-PHOTO / SYS-PLANT**: bubbles-vs-lamp-distance; CO₂ indicator colour by time of day; oil on leaf surfaces blocks tiny openings; starch test after blocking CO₂/light; ringing (remove food tubes: swelling above cut, roots starve, fruit smaller; remove water tubes: leaves wilt); day vs night gas exchange; grass under object turns yellow.
- **ENE-HEAT**: heat gain/loss direction; conductor vs insulator (air trapped in cotton/cup sleeve); cooling curves for different volumes or insulation; expansion (tiles need gaps, air bubbles grow, lid loosens in hot water); contraction; heat vs temperature.
- **CYC-MATTER**: air volume arithmetic in containers/syringes (air compresses, water does not); air has mass (balance); no definite shape for liquids; melting/boiling points from state tables; volume by displacement.
- **ENE-LIGHT**: shadow size vs object/torch/screen positions; light sensor readings; reflection off glass and mirrors; opaque/translucent/transparent cut-outs → dark vs light shadows.
- **DIV**: classification charts/flowcharts; reptile vs mammal (body covering, reproduction); flowering vs non-flowering (seeds vs spores); bacteria growth vs temperature/oxygen; material property tables.
- **CYC-LIFE**: 3- vs 4-stage insects; which day has both pupa and nymph; life-cycle duration bar graphs; plant growth sequence.
