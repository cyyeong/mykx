# Memory strategies (evidence-based, low pressure)

The techniques below are the ones with the strongest research support for retention in
children: **retrieval practice, spacing, interleaving, dual coding, elaboration/self-explanation,
concrete examples, and errorful learning with immediate feedback.** They are wrapped so they feel
like short games, never like drills. A learner is never labelled; they are told the brain keeps
what it *uses*.

## A. The core loop: "Ask, don't tell"
Every fact is *retrieved*, not re-read. Pattern for any concept card:
1. Ask the mini-check question first (even if the learner cannot answer). Wait 10–15 s.
2. If stuck, give a **cue**, not the answer (first letter, the picture, "think of the cold can").
3. If still stuck, give the answer and immediately ask the learner to **say it back in their own words**, then
   re-ask the same question 2–3 minutes later inside another task.
4. The app marks the item as `learning`. It comes back tomorrow, then in 3 days, then in 7.

## B. Spacing schedule inside a 12-day window
- Day n: learn/repair an item → Day n+1 quick re-test (1 min) → Day n+3 → Day n+7 → exam eve.
- Every session begins with a **3-minute warm-up of yesterday's items** (retrieval, not review).
- Keep a rolling **"Five to keep" card**: five facts currently in repair. New facts enter only when
  one graduates (answered correctly on two spaced days).

## C. Interleave, don't block
After the diagnostic, sessions mix 2–3 topics (e.g. heat + water cycle + one food-web question)
because the exam mixes them and because switching forces the learner to *identify the concept* — the
exact skill PSLE tests. Blocked practice feels easier but fades faster.

## D. Dual coding — every idea gets a picture and a sentence
- Draw the concept once (heat arrows from hot to cold; food-chain arrows; circuit loop).
- Have the learner draw it back from memory the next day (stick figures fine).
- Use real diagrams, not text.

## E. Elaboration prompts ("why?" and "what if?")
After any right answer: "Why does that work?" / "What would happen if it were metal instead?" /
"Where else have we seen this?" Two follow-ups maximum; then move on.

## F. Concrete anchors — one everyday object per abstract idea
| Idea | Anchor |
|---|---|
| Heat flows hot → cold | ice-cream melting in your hand: your hand *loses* heat |
| Condensation | cold Milo can "sweating" at the void deck |
| Evaporation factors | school uniform dries faster on a windy day, spread out |
| Series vs parallel | Christmas lights that all die together vs house lights that don't |
| Prey/predator graphs | fewer fish → hungry cats → fewer cats later |
| GPE → KE | playground slide: highest at the top, fastest at the bottom |
| Friction is useful | why socks slip on tiles but not on carpet |
| Air occupies space | bubble tea cup lid pops up when pressed |
| Elastic spring force | hair tie stretched further pulls back harder |
| Tiny openings blocked | breathing through a plastic bag on your face |

## G. Mnemonics (only where a *list* must be recalled)
- **Photosynthesis inputs "CLW → SO"**: Carbon dioxide, Light, Water → Sugar, Oxygen. ("Clever plants Sing Out".)
- **Germination "WOT"**: Water, Oxygen, suitable Temperature. ("Seeds ask: WOT do I need?")
- **Dispersal "WAWS"**: Wind, Animals, Water, Splitting.
- **Reproduction order "G-P-F-D"**: Germinate, Pollinate, Fertilise, Disperse ("Good Plants Feed Deer").
- **4-stage insects "BB-MMH"**: Butterfly, Beetle, Mosquito, Moth, Housefly (they have pupae). Everything with a *nymph* is 3-stage: cockroach, grasshopper, dragonfly.
- **Forces on any object "G-F-M-S"**: Gravity always; Friction if touching; Magnetic if magnet; Spring if squashed/stretched.
- **Magnetic metals "I-S-N-C"**: Iron, Steel, Nickel, Cobalt ("I See No Copper").
- **Digestive path "Mouth Goes Straight to Small and Large"**: mouth, gullet, stomach, small intestine, large intestine.
- **Blood loop "Body → Heart → Lungs → Heart → Body"** with "CO₂-rich" on the way to lungs and "O₂-rich" on the way back.
- **Exhaled air "COW"**: more Carbon dioxide, less Oxygen, more Water vapour (and warmer).
- **Variables "C-M-K"**: Changed (I decide), Measured (I record), Kept same (fair).
- **Booklet B "CER"**: Claim, Evidence, Reasoning.

## H. Error log = the real revision list
Keep an "Oops book" (the learner record's error log): question type, what the learner wrote, the marking point missed,
the corrected sentence in their own words. Re-test only from the Oops book on Day 10–11. Every
error is framed as "found a trap before the exam did" — say this every time.

## I. Low-pressure rules the tutor follows
1. One question on screen at a time; no timers until Day 8, and then only for MCQ sets of 5.
2. Sessions 20–30 min; a 2-minute break after ~12 minutes; stop on a win.
3. Praise the *process* ("you underlined the changed variable — that is exactly what got the mark").
4. When the learner is wrong, respond with curiosity: "Interesting — what made you pick that?" Then locate the
   misconception, fix it, and give an easier sibling question so the session ends with success.
5. Never stack three hard questions. Difficulty ladder: 1 easy warm-up → 2 medium → 1 stretch → 1 easy close.
6. Ask the learner to teach the concept back to a "younger cousin" — the teach-back is the retention test.
7. Keep language plain; introduce one new keyword at a time and use it three times in the next five minutes.
8. If the learner says "I forgot", reply "That's fine — remembering after forgetting is how it sticks. Cue: …".

## J. Signs of overload (pause, switch to a cheap-win topic or stop)
Long silences, "I don't know" three times in a row, rushing answers, asking how many left, tearful
or irritable replies. Respond by shrinking the task (one MCQ, then a light classification question) and
ending early with a genuine success.
