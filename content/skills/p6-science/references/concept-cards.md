# Concept cards: every fact, in the words the marker wants

Each card: **Must-know** (say it exactly like this) · **Watch-outs** (misconceptions that lose marks) ·
**Mini-check** (a 20-second retrieval question to ask the learner). Wording follows the 2023 syllabus
(see `syllabus.md`). Sources: MOE/SEAB documents, recent school papers and their answer keys (private
reference), and published analyses of recent PSLE papers.

## DIV-LIV — Living things & classification
**Must-know**
- Living things need air, food and water; grow; respond to changes around them; reproduce; die.
- Plants: **flowering** (reproduce by seeds; bear flowers and fruits) vs **non-flowering** (reproduce by spores, e.g. fern, moss, or by seeds without flowers, e.g. conifer). All plants make their own food.
- Animal groups: mammals (hair/fur, give birth to live young, suckle young) · birds (feathers, lay eggs, wings) · reptiles (dry scaly skin, lay eggs) · amphibians (moist skin, live on land and in water, lay eggs in water) · fish (scales, gills, fins, lay eggs) · insects (3 body parts, 6 legs, hard outer covering).
- Bat = mammal; whale/dolphin = mammal; penguin = bird; turtle/crocodile = reptile; frog = amphibian.
- Fungi (mushroom, mould, yeast) and bacteria are living things; some are micro-organisms. Bacteria grow faster when warm, moist and with food/oxygen; cold (fridge) slows growth; airtight/less oxygen slows growth; cooking kills.
**Watch-outs** — "It moves" ≠ living (cars move). Bats are not birds. Reptile vs mammal distinguisher: **body covering** or **method of reproduction** (not legs, not habitat).
**Mini-check** — "Give me two differences between a reptile and a mammal, and one thing that both do."

## DIV-MAT — Materials
**Must-know** — Properties: strength, flexibility, hardness, waterproof/absorbs water, transparent/translucent/opaque, conductor/insulator of heat, conductor/insulator of electricity, floats/sinks. A test that loads something until it breaks tests **strength**; bending tests **flexibility**.
**Watch-outs** — Choose material by the property the *use* needs (car cover: opaque + waterproof + flexible). Metal is a good conductor of heat AND electricity; rubber/plastic/wood/air are poor conductors of both.
**Mini-check** — "A food-delivery box must keep food warm and not tear. Which two properties?"

## CYC-LIFE — Life cycles
**Must-know**
- 3-stage: egg → **nymph** (young looks like small wingless adult) → adult. Cockroach, grasshopper, dragonfly.
- 4-stage: egg → **larva** (looks nothing like adult, no wings, eats a lot, grows) → **pupa** (does not feed; mass stays about constant) → adult. Butterfly, moth, mosquito, housefly, beetle (mealworm).
- Frog: egg → tadpole → adult. Plant: seed → seedling/young plant → adult plant (flowers → fruits → seeds).
- Laying many eggs raises the chance that some survive to adulthood.
**Watch-outs** — "Nymph" ≠ "larva". Wings appear only in the adult. Read life-cycle *duration* graphs bar by bar.
**Mini-check** — "Which insect has a pupa: cockroach or mosquito? What does a pupa look like?"

## CYC-REPRO — Reproduction in plants and humans
**Must-know**
- Order: germination → growth → **pollination** (pollen from anther to stigma) → **fertilisation** (male reproductive cell in pollen fuses with female cell/egg in the ovule, inside the ovary) → fruit and seed formation → **seed dispersal** → germination.
- Flower: anther (produces pollen = male reproductive cells) held up by filament; stigma (receives pollen, often sticky), style, ovary (contains ovules = female cells; becomes the **fruit**); ovule → **seed**; petals attract insects.
- Pollination clues: insects → large bright petals, scent, nectar, sticky/rough pollen, sticky stigma; wind → small dull petals, light powdery pollen, feathery stigma; flies → smell of rotting meat.
- Dispersal: wind (wing-like or hair-like structures, light) · animals (fleshy/juicy fruit eaten, seeds passed out in droppings; hooks stick to fur) · water (fibrous husk, air spaces, floats) · splitting/explosive action (pod dries, splits; seeds land near parent). Dispersal **reduces overcrowding and competition for light, water, mineral salts and space**.
- Germination needs **sufficient water, oxygen (air) and a suitable temperature**. Light is NOT needed to germinate (needed later for photosynthesis). Seed leaves shrink as they supply food; seedling mass rises.
- Humans: testes produce sperm (male cells); ovaries produce eggs (female cells); fertilisation in the fallopian tube/womb region; fertilised egg develops in the **womb (uterus)**. Cutting one fallopian tube: eggs from the other side can still be fertilised.
- Living things are made of cells (that is all you need about cells). Traits are passed from parents to young.
**Watch-outs** — Fruit comes from the **ovary**, seed from the **ovule**. Splitting fruits land NEAR the parent (short-distance dispersal). "State the characteristic of the fruit" — describe the fruit (pod-like/wing-like), not the method.
**Mini-check** — "Pollination is… Fertilisation is… (fill both in one sentence each)."

## CYC-MATTER — Matter
**Must-know**
- Matter has mass and occupies space (volume). Solid: definite shape + volume. Liquid: no definite shape, definite volume. Gas: no definite shape or volume; **can be compressed**; spreads to fill its container.
- Air has mass (inflated ball heavier on a balance) and occupies space (balloon in bottle will not inflate; water cannot enter an upturned glass).
- Container arithmetic: water keeps its volume; air fills the rest. Pumping air in → air compresses; volumes of water unchanged. Syringe with water + air: only the air part compresses.
- Melting point = freezing point. Below it: solid; between melting and boiling point: liquid; above boiling: gas. Read state tables to bound the points.
- Volume of an irregular solid = rise in water level (displacement); object must be fully submerged and must sink.
**Watch-outs** — Removing air from a bottle reduces the **mass of air**, not its volume. Liquids do not compress.
**Mini-check** — "Jar 500 cm³ with 100 cm³ water. Pump in 50 cm³ air. Volumes now?" (air 400, water 100)

## CYC-WATER — Water cycle & changes of state
**Must-know**
- Evaporation: water **gains heat** from the surroundings and changes into water vapour at any temperature. Faster with larger exposed surface area, more wind, higher temperature, drier air.
- Boiling: at 100 °C, bubbles of water vapour form throughout; temperature stays at 100 °C while boiling.
- Condensation: water vapour (in the air) touches a **cooler surface/air**, **loses heat** and changes into water droplets. Mist, "white smoke", fog, droplets on a cold can, on the inside of a hot cup's lid.
- Melting: ice gains heat → water; stays at 0 °C while melting (mixture of ice and water). Freezing: water loses heat → ice at 0 °C.
- Temperature–time graph: flat line = change of state in progress; steep rise after adding a heat source; sudden drop = something cold added.
- Water cycle: evaporation from seas/lakes (and water vapour from plants and animals) → condensation into clouds → rain → collects in seas, rivers, ground.
- Pollution: rubbish harms sea creatures; fertilisers/waste cause algae growth; clean water matters.
**Watch-outs** — Droplets on a **cold** object come from **water vapour in the surrounding air**, never from inside the container. Say "loses heat" (not "cold enters"). Mist disappears because droplets **gain heat and evaporate**. Water vapour is invisible; mist is liquid.
**Mini-check** — "Cold can outside on a hot day: where do the droplets come from and what happened to the vapour?"

## SYS-PLANT — Plant system
**Must-know**
- Roots: anchor the plant; absorb **water and mineral salts**. Stem: holds the plant upright; carries water and food in tubes. Leaves: make food (photosynthesis); tiny openings let gases in/out and water vapour escape. Flowers: reproduction.
- **Water-carrying tubes**: water & mineral salts from roots to all parts. **Food-carrying tubes**: food (sugar) from leaves to all parts (up to fruits, down to roots).
- Ringing experiment (removing a ring of bark = food-carrying tubes): food piles up **above** the cut (swelling), parts **below** get no food (roots starve, plant eventually dies; fruit below the cut gets less food). If water-carrying tubes are removed, leaves **above** wilt (no water).
- Loss of water vapour through tiny openings is replaced by water absorbed by roots.
**Watch-outs** — Roots do NOT absorb food. Fruit gets bigger when food is trapped above a cut just below it. Use the plain names for tubes.
**Mini-check** — "Bark ring removed below all the leaves. Where does swelling appear and why?"

## SYS-HUMAN — Human systems
**Must-know**
- Digestive: mouth (teeth cut/grind; saliva starts digesting) → gullet (pushes food to stomach) → stomach (mixes food with digestive juices) → small intestine (digestion completed; **digested food absorbed into the blood**) → large intestine (**absorbs water**) → undigested food leaves the body. Digestive juices in mouth, stomach, small intestine. Digestion = breaking food down into simpler substances the body can absorb.
- Respiratory: nose → windpipe → lungs. Takes in oxygen, gives out carbon dioxide (**gaseous exchange** in lungs). Exhaled air: less oxygen, more carbon dioxide, more water vapour, warmer; nitrogen unchanged.
- **Respiration** (in every living cell, all the time): digested food + oxygen → energy released (+ carbon dioxide + water). Breathing ≠ respiration.
- Circulatory: heart pumps blood through blood vessels to all parts. Blood **carries** oxygen and digested food to all parts and carries carbon dioxide and waste away. Blood from lungs to heart to body is **oxygen-rich**; blood from body back to heart to lungs is **carbon-dioxide-rich**. Blood leaving the small intestine has the most digested food.
- Exercise: muscles respire faster to release **more energy** → heart pumps **faster** and breathing rate rises → **more oxygen and digested food** reach muscles and **more carbon dioxide** is removed. Heart rate steady = same exercise intensity.
- Skeletal system gives the body shape and protects organs; muscles move bones.
- Systems work together: digestive gives digested food → circulatory carries it; respiratory gives oxygen → circulatory carries it → cells respire.
**Watch-outs** — Comparative words are marked: "more", "faster". Lungs are not part of the circulatory system. Fish exchange gases at gills; plants at tiny openings.
**Mini-check** — "Trace a drop of blood from the legs back to the legs: legs →? →? →? → legs, saying rich/poor in oxygen at each step."

## SYS-ELEC — Electrical system
**Must-know**
- Current flows only in a **closed circuit** (battery, wires, components, closed switch, no gaps). Open circuit → nothing works.
- Conductors of electricity: metals (copper, aluminium, steel, iron), graphite. Insulators: plastic, rubber, wood, glass, paper, air.
- **Series** (one loop): more bulbs → each dimmer; one bulb fuses → all go off; adding batteries in series → brighter. Switch anywhere controls everything.
- **Parallel** (branches): each branch independent; bulbs stay as bright when more branches are added; one branch fusing leaves others lit; a switch on a branch controls only that branch; a switch in the main loop controls all.
- Circuit tester: bulb lights ⇒ the two points are connected by a conductor.
- Electromagnet: coil of wire around an iron rod + closed circuit → temporary magnet; stronger with more coils or more batteries; switches off when circuit opens. Iron core loses magnetism when current stops; steel would keep some.
- Safety: insulate wires (rubber/plastic protects people from shock); don't overload one socket; switch off before changing a bulb; don't use damaged wires.
**Watch-outs** — Two batteries facing opposite ways cancel. A bulb's metal casing and metal tip both need connections. "Current is used up" is wrong. Bulbs "fuse".
**Mini-check** — "Three bulbs: two on one branch, one on another. Which is brightest? What happens to the others if the lone bulb fuses?"

## INT-ENV — Interactions within the environment
**Must-know**
- Food chain arrow points **from food to eater** (direction of energy flow). Producer (plant, makes food using light) → prey/consumer → predator. Sun is the ultimate energy source, passed on indirectly.
- **Population** = organisms of the same kind living in the same place at the same time. **Community** = all the populations living together in a habitat. **Habitat** = the place where an organism lives.
- Predict change: if X's food decreases → X decreases (less food). If X's predator decreases → X increases (fewer eaten). Second-order: a predator losing one prey **eats more of another** prey. Populations rarely change instantly — say "after some time / weeks".
- Reading population graphs: the line that **falls** while another **rises** is the **prey**; the riser is the predator. Two organisms both eating the same food compete.
- Adaptations: **structural** (body part: webbed feet push more water; sharp curved claws grip prey; long thin beak reaches nectar; flat strong beak cracks shells; dark feathers camouflage on dark rocks; hairy leaves trap air; slippery pitcher; white inner eggshell must be hidden from predators) vs **behavioural** (what it does: hunts at night; migrates; carries eggshells away; huddles). Always link **feature → function → survival** (get food / avoid predator / reproduce / avoid heat or cold).
- Man's impact: deforestation → loss of habitat, soil erosion, more CO₂ (fewer plants removing it) → global warming → melting polar ice, rising seas; burning fossil fuels → CO₂ and polluted gases; fertilisers/rubbish → water pollution, algae growth; overfishing/hunting → extinction. Conservation: reduce, reuse, recycle, protected areas, replant.
**Watch-outs** — Never write "the food chain breaks". Write which population changes, which direction, and why (food or predator). Name the *survival benefit*, not just the feature.
**Mini-check** — "Grass → grasshopper → lizard → hawk. Grasshoppers are poisoned. What happens to lizards after a few weeks, and why? Two steps."

## INT-FORCE — Forces & magnets
**Must-know**
- A force is a push or a pull. Effects: start/stop movement, speed up/slow down, change direction, change shape. Light being blocked or reflected is NOT a force effect.
- **Gravitational force**: the pull that Earth exerts on all objects — pulls objects down, keeps them on the ground; acts even when the object is not moving; does not change with height at PSLE level (graph is a flat line).
- **Frictional force**: produced when two surfaces rub against each other; acts opposite to the direction of motion (or intended motion); slows things down, produces heat and wears surfaces; rougher surfaces → more friction; **heavier object pressing down → more friction**; friction is NOT affected by surface area; reduce with lubricant/oil, wheels/rollers, smooth surfaces. Friction is also useful: grip, walking, brakes.
- **Elastic spring force**: push or pull by a stretched or compressed object trying to return to its original shape. Same spring + heavier load → stretches/compresses more → exerts more force. Springs return to original length when the load is removed (unless overstretched).
- **Magnetic force**: attraction (unlike poles or magnet ↔ magnetic material: iron, steel, nickel, cobalt) or repulsion (like poles). Only a **magnet repels**; attraction alone means "magnet or magnetic material". Non-magnetic: copper, aluminium, gold, plastic, wood. Poles are strongest; strength falls with distance (measured by how far away a clip is attracted, or how many clips held). Make a magnet by **stroking** (one direction, one pole, many times) or the **electrical method** (coil + current). Iron → temporary magnet; steel → longer-lasting. Demagnetise by heating, dropping/hammering.
- Balanced forces: spring force up = gravitational force down → stationary. Object on slope stays still when friction ≥ pull of gravity along the slope; add mass? more friction *and* more pull — push it or tilt the ramp instead.
**Watch-outs** — On any object always list gravity first. A magnet on a whiteboard: friction (holds it up), gravity, magnetic attraction. Gravitational force ≠ gravitational potential energy.
**Mini-check** — "Name all forces on a book sliding to a stop on a table. Which one stops it?"

## ENE-PHOTO — Photosynthesis (energy in food)
**Must-know**
- Plants make food in leaves: **carbon dioxide + water —(light)→ sugar/food + oxygen**. Light energy is trapped by the green parts (chlorophyll, optional word). Food stored as starch (iodine turns blue-black).
- Needs **light, carbon dioxide and water**. Remove light → no photosynthesis; grass under a bench yellows. Block CO₂ (absorbing solution) → no starch made.
- More light (lamp nearer) → faster photosynthesis → more oxygen bubbles per minute. Plants also **respire all the time** (use oxygen, give out CO₂). In daylight photosynthesis > respiration → net CO₂ falls, O₂ rises; at night only respiration → CO₂ rises. Sealed-jar graphs follow this. CO₂ indicators: more CO₂ → yellow, less CO₂ → purple.
- Plants are the source of energy for animals (indirectly from the Sun).
**Watch-outs** — Plants do NOT stop respiring in the day. Oil on leaf surfaces blocks tiny openings → less gaseous exchange (underside has more openings). "Food" for plants = sugar they made, not soil/fertiliser (mineral salts help growth but are not food).
**Mini-check** — "Word equation for photosynthesis; what does the plant do with the sugar at night?"

## ENE-CONV — Energy forms & conversion
**Must-know**
- Energy = ability to do work. Forms: **kinetic** (moving), **potential** (position or state: gravitational PE at a height; elastic PE in stretched/compressed springs; chemical PE in food, batteries, fuel), heat, light, sound, electrical.
- Energy is **not created or destroyed, only converted** from one form to another. Some always becomes heat (and sound) — so a bouncing ball never returns to its original height; a ramp with friction gives less KE at the bottom.
- Higher position or more mass → more GPE. Faster or heavier → more KE. Falling: GPE → KE. Rolling to a stop: KE → heat (+sound) by friction.
- Standard chains: torch: chemical PE (battery) → electrical → light + heat. Toy car: chemical PE → electrical → kinetic + sound. Wind turbine: kinetic (wind) → kinetic (blades) → electrical → light/heat. Dam: GPE (water) → KE (falling water) → KE (turbine) → electrical. Water wheel: GPE → KE → KE. Wound spring: elastic PE → KE (+ heat/sound). Solar panel: light → electrical. Photosynthesis: light → chemical PE in food.
- Renewable (wind, solar, water) vs non-renewable (fossil fuels: petroleum, coal, natural gas).
**Watch-outs** — Write full chains with arrows and name the object at each step: "GPE (ball) → KE (ball) → heat + sound". Say "converted", not "used up". Oiled slope → less friction → **less heat, more KE**, same starting PE.
**Mini-check** — "Ball dropped from 1 m bounces to 60 cm. Explain with energy in two sentences."

## ENE-HEAT — Heat
**Must-know**
- Heat is a form of energy; **temperature** is how hot or cold something is (measured in °C with a thermometer). Same temperature, larger volume → more heat energy.
- Heat **flows from the hotter to the colder object/place until both reach the same temperature**. Hot drink cools: it loses heat to the cooler air. Ice melts: it gains heat from the warmer air/plate. Warm water in fridge: loses heat until it reaches fridge temperature.
- **Good conductors** (metals) gain and lose heat quickly; **poor conductors/insulators** (air, wood, plastic, cloth, styrofoam, water) slowly. Trapped air in cotton wool, fur, cup sleeves, double walls slows heat flow **in both directions** (keeps hot things hot, cold things cold).
- Heating → **expansion**; cooling → **contraction** (solids, liquids, gases; gases most). Gaps between tiles/rails; metal lid loosens in hot water; air bubbles enlarge on a hot day; liquid in a thermometer rises.
- Cooling curves: bigger volume or insulated container → slower drop; all end at room temperature (levelling off).
**Watch-outs** — Never say "cold moves". Say which object **gains/loses heat**. Boiling time equal despite different conductivity → volumes differed. Poor conductor gets hot on one side only because heat spreads slowly (the "otah/banana-leaf" 2025 PSLE question).
**Mini-check** — "Metal spoon in hot soup feels hot at the handle after a while. Direction of heat flow? Why does a wooden spoon not?"

## ENE-LIGHT — Light
**Must-know**
- Light travels in **straight lines**; we see an object when light from it (its own or reflected) enters our eyes. Mirrors and shiny/smooth surfaces reflect most light; glass reflects some and lets most through (faint reflection).
- Opaque blocks all light → dark shadow. Translucent lets some light through → lighter/blurred shadow. Transparent lets almost all through → no shadow.
- Shadow **bigger** when the object is nearer the light source or further from the screen (or torch nearer object); **smaller** when the object is nearer the screen or further from the torch. Shadow shape follows the object's outline facing the light.
- Light sensor reading drops when more light is blocked (object placed in path or moved nearer the lamp) or the source is further away.
**Watch-outs** — Light does not come from the eye. A shadow needs a screen/surface to fall on.
**Mini-check** — "Torch fixed. To make the shadow of a toy on the wall smaller, move the toy where?"
