# PSLE Science: syllabus, format and wording

Checked against SEAB's "PSLE Science 0009, for examination from 2026", MOE's *Science Teaching and
Learning Syllabus, Primary Three to Six* (2023 syllabus, updated Jan 2026) and school curriculum
briefings. Where a claim comes from a tuition-centre comparison rather than an official document, it is
marked *(reported)*. The learning outcomes, item by item, are in `syllabus-map.md`.

## Exam day and format (SEAB)
- **One sitting, 1 h 45 min.** The learner's exam date is in their profile.
- **Booklet A:** 30 MCQs × 2 marks = **60 marks**. Four options each. Shaded on OAS.
- **Booklet B:** 10–11 structured questions, 2–5 marks each = **40 marks**. Written in booklet.
- Total 100 marks. AL1 ≈ 90+. Every question must be attempted.
- **New in 2026:** MCQ weighting rose from 56 → 60 marks; open-ended fell from 44 → 40. Up to
  **two pairs of linked MCQs** may share one scenario (seen in recent school papers). A misread scenario costs 4 marks, not 2.
- Booklet B is *(reported)* split roughly evenly between life-science and physical-science contexts.
- This is the **first PSLE cohort examined on the MOE 2023 Primary Science syllabus.** Pre-2023
  assessment books contain out-of-syllabus material and old vocabulary.

## Assessment objectives (SEAB)
1. **Knowledge with understanding** — facts, concepts, principles.
2. **Application and scientific inquiry** — apply concepts; make predictions and hypotheses;
   interpret and analyse information; evaluate observations, information and methods;
   communicate explanations and reasoning (words, diagrams, tables, graphs).
The paper leans on AO2: experiment contexts, data tables, graphs and unfamiliar real-life
scenarios dominate. Pure recall is rare.

## Themes and topics by level (all P3–P6 content is examinable)
| Theme | Topic (2023 wording) | Level | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diversity | Diversity of living and non-living things (characteristics, classification; plants incl. flowering/non-flowering; animals: mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fish, insects; fungi and bacteria as living things) | P3 | Classification keys/flowcharts common. Recall of named living things and their traits is not required: questions give the characteristics |
| Diversity | Diversity of materials (properties → uses: wood, metal, ceramic, rubber, glass, plastic, fabric) | P3 | strength (loads without breaking), flexibility (bends without breaking), waterproof (does not absorb water), transparency (lets most/some/no light through), float/sink. Heat and electrical conduction are taught under Heat (P4) and Electrical system (P5) |
| Cycles | Life cycles of plants and animals (chicken, cockroach, frog, grasshopper, beetle, butterfly, mosquito) | P3 | 3-stage (egg–young/nymph–adult) vs 4-stage (egg–larva–pupa–adult); frog; plant seed→young→adult |
| Cycles | Matter (anything that has mass and occupies space; three states by shape & volume; measuring mass and volume with apparatus; air occupies space, has mass, can be compressed) | P4 | melting point and boiling point are Water (P5) terms, applied to other substances in tables |
| Cycles | Water (evaporation, condensation, boiling, melting, freezing; water cycle; temperature–time graphs showing constant temperature during a change of state; water pollution incl. algae growth) | P5 | Very high frequency |
| Cycles | Reproduction in plants and humans (a cell is a basic unit of life; traits passed from parents to offspring; pollination, fertilisation, seed dispersal, germination; plants reproduce by spores or seeds; human fertilisation: sperm fuses with egg, fertilised egg develops in the womb) | P5 | "A cell is a basic unit of life" survives here — nothing more about cells |
| Systems | Plant system: parts & functions; water-carrying and food-carrying tubes; tiny openings on leaves | P4/P5 | Ringing experiments frequent |
| Systems | Human system: the body's systems and their functions (P4, skeletal & muscular as an overview only); digestive (P4); air is made up of gases such as nitrogen, carbon dioxide, oxygen and water vapour; respiratory and circulatory; systems working together; how plants, fish and humans take in oxygen; transport in plants vs humans (P5) | P4/P5 | oxygen-rich / carbon-dioxide-rich blood |
| Systems | Electrical system (closed/open circuits, conductors/insulators, series vs parallel, switches, safety) | P5 | Electromagnet contexts appear inside circuit questions |
| Interactions | Interaction of forces: magnetic (P3, incl. making a magnet by the stroke and electrical methods), gravitational, frictional, elastic spring (P6; objects have weight because of the gravitational force) | P3/P6 | Effects of forces; contact/non-contact |
| Interactions | Interactions within the environment (factors affecting survival: temperature, light, water, food, producers/consumers/decomposers; food chains/webs; organism, population, community; habitats; adaptations structural/behavioural; man's positive and negative impact) | P6 | Merged heading; all sub-topics still tested |
| Energy | Energy forms and uses: light (seen when an object reflects light or is a light source; travels in straight lines; shadow when light is completely or partially blocked; shadow variables) | P4 | The terms transparent/translucent/opaque and the law of reflection are not required |
| Energy | Energy forms and uses: heat (flows hotter→colder until same temperature; conductors/insulators; expansion/contraction; heat ≠ temperature) | P4 | |
| Energy | Energy forms and uses: photosynthesis (light energy + carbon dioxide + water → sugar + oxygen; living things need energy from respiration; the Sun is our primary source of energy; how plants and animals obtain energy) | P6 | Chlorophyll is not in the learning outcomes |
| Energy | Energy conversion (kinetic, potential, light, electrical, sound, heat; most energy resources come from the Sun; conserving energy) | P6 | Very high frequency. The terms chemical, gravitational and elastic potential energy are not required: "potential energy" earns the mark |

**Removed for 2026:** *Cells* as a standalone topic (cell parts — nucleus, cytoplasm, membrane,
wall, chloroplast — and plant vs animal cell comparisons). Skip any such question in old
materials. Also not required, as the 2023 syllabus notes say: xylem/phloem
and stomata as terms, artery/vein/capillary, alveoli, heart chambers and valves, foetal
development and the umbilical cord, transpiration pull (full list in `syllabus-map.md`).

## Current vocabulary that earns the mark *(reported textbook comparison — treat as best practice)*
| Old habit | Say this instead |
|---|---|
| xylem / phloem | **water-carrying tubes / food-carrying tubes** |
| stomata | **tiny openings** (stomata still accepted) |
| electrical current | **current** |
| bulb "blew" | bulb **fuses** |
| "transports" substances (circulatory) | **carries** substances |
| oxygenated / deoxygenated | **oxygen-rich / carbon-dioxide-rich** blood |
| friction "opposes motion when surfaces are in contact" | friction is produced **when two objects/surfaces rub against each other**; acts opposite to motion |
| gravity "attraction between objects" | gravitational force is the **pull that Earth exerts on all objects**; keeps things on the ground, pulls objects down |
| spring force "by a spring" | **elastic spring force** — the push or pull exerted by any stretched or compressed object (spring, rubber band, ball) |
| heat "makes us feel warm" | heat is a form of energy; **flows from hotter to colder** object/place **until both reach the same temperature** |
| potential energy "stored" | energy an object has because of its **position or state** (also called stored energy) |
| photosynthesis needs light, CO₂, water **and chlorophyll** | needs **light, carbon dioxide and water** (chlorophyll optional) |
| "minerals" | **mineral salts** |
| "prevents overcrowding" | dispersal **reduces overcrowding**; competition for **light, water, mineral salts and space** |
| germination needs air, water, warmth | **sufficient water, oxygen (air), suitable (warm) temperature** |
| wing-like / feathery | **wing-like or hair-like structures** |
| stomach "churns" | stomach **mixes** food |
| large intestine absorbs water and minerals | large intestine **absorbs water** |
| digested food absorbed into bloodstream | absorbed into the **blood** |

## Marks per theme (school planning documents)
Diversity 5–10%, Cycles 20–25%, Systems 15–25%, Interactions 25–30%, Energy 15–20%.
Life science ≈ physical science ≈ 45–55% each. These match the school-paper statistics (see `topic-stats.md`).
