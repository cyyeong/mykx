# G3 English blueprint (SEAB K300) and lower-secondary school papers

Source for the papers and marks: SEAB, *Secondary Education Certificate, G3 English
Language, Syllabus K300* (for examination in 2027; listed on SEAB's "G3 Syllabuses for
School Candidates" page, with 1184 as the code for 2026 and earlier). The pacing and
archetypes are this pack's advice.

## The G3 English papers (SEAB K300)

| Paper | Section | What candidates do | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Writing (1 h 50 min, 70 marks, 35%) | A: Editing | identify and edit grammatical errors in a text of continuous prose of not more than 250 words; punctuation and spelling are not tested | 10 |
| | B: Situational Writing | 250–350 words on a given situation involving a visual text | 30 |
| | C: Continuous Writing | 350–500 words on one of four topics | 30 |
| 2 Comprehension (1 h 50 min, 50 marks, 35%) | A | questions on Texts 1 and 2, one of which includes visuals: comprehension, use of visuals, language for effect | 5 |
| | B | questions on Text 3, a narrative or recount: comprehension, vocabulary in context, language for effect | 20 |
| | C | questions on Text 4, a non-narrative text: comprehension, vocabulary in context, language for effect, and a summary of about 80 words (excluding the introductory words provided) | 25 |
| 3 Listening (about 45 min, 30 marks, 10%) | A and B | listening tasks; note-taking | 22 + 8 |
| 4 Oral Communication (about 20 min, 30 marks, 20%) | Parts 1 and 2 | planned response to a video clip; spoken interaction | 15 + 15 |

Texts 3 and 4 together are about 1,200 words. The summary is judged for content and for
style; SEAB's summary style band runs to 7 marks (own words and structures, organisation).
SEAB does not state the content marks separately; this pack assumes about 8, so the summary
is about 15 of Section C's 25 marks.

Paper 2's assessment objectives (SEAB): show understanding of written and multimodal
texts at the literal, inferential and evaluative levels, including the use of language for
effect; identify main ideas and details as well as synthesise and summarise information.

## Lower-secondary school papers

Sec 1 and Sec 2 tests are set by each school against the EL Syllabus 2020's learning
outcomes; the syllabus says lower-secondary assessment "could cover a more varied range of
learning outcomes" than upper secondary. Schools' published exam formats commonly follow
the shape above (editing, a visual text, a narrative and a non-narrative passage with a
summary), often with shorter texts and summaries, and some add other language exercises. If the
learner's profile records the school's own format, follow it for school tests.

## Pacing

For a full Paper 2 (1 h 50 min):

| Minutes | Do |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | Section A: visual text (5 marks) |
| 10–50 | Section B: read the narrative once fully, then answer |
| 50–100 | Section C: questions first, then the summary (plan points, then write) |
| 100–110 | The final check in `answer-strategies.md` §7 |

Editing on Paper 1 takes about 10–15 minutes; do it first, while fresh.

## Archetypes that repeat

**Editing:** tense in a narrative (*last month … spent*); agreement with *each of, the number
of, neither … nor*; fixed prepositions (*rely on, interested in*); relative pronouns (*who*
for people after a comma); noun number after *one of the*; verb forms (*agree to do*,
*avoid doing*); pronoun case and reflexives; connectors used twice (*although … but*).

**Visual text:** purpose and audience; how a picture or layout supports the message;
persuasive techniques (rhetorical questions, imperatives, testimonials, statistics,
inclusive *you* and *we*).

**Narrative:** literal retrieval; inference about motives; vocabulary in context; language
for effect (simile, metaphor, personification, word choice); a character's feelings shown
through actions; what the ending suggests.

**Non-narrative:** the writer's view and purpose; explaining a claim; vocabulary in
context; the effect of word choice and of the closing line.

**Summary:** separate main points from examples; rewrite in own words; join the points in
continuous prose within about 80 words after the given opening.
