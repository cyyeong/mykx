# How to answer: editing, comprehension and summary

Each routine has a short name Kix can call out ("Quote, Meaning, Effect"). Teach one at a
time, practise it on three items, then mix. At Sec 2 most lost marks in comprehension and
summary come from method: unexplained quotes, missing evidence and lifted sentences.

## 1. Editing: READ, SPOT, FIX

1. **Read** the whole text once for meaning and its main tense.
2. **Spot**, line by line, in this order:
   * **Verbs:** tense against time markers (*last month, in 1998, since*); verb forms
     after *agree to, avoid -ing, let + base*; passive forms.
   * **Agreement:** find the real subject (cover *of the bundles*); *each, every, the
     number of* are singular; with *neither … nor* the nearer subject decides.
   * **Small words:** fixed prepositions (*rely on, interested in, despite* with no *of*),
     relative pronouns (*who* for people after a comma), articles, noun number after
     *one of the*.
   * **Joining words:** never two connectors for one link (*although … but*).
3. **Fix**: write only the corrected word. Spelling and punctuation are not tested in this
   section, so do not "correct" them.

If a line seems correct, read it once more aloud in your head; in practice texts a line
may have no error at all.

## 2. Visual text: PURPOSE, AUDIENCE, TECHNIQUE

1. **Purpose:** what does the text want the reader to do or think? Look at the title, the
   call to action and the contact details.
2. **Audience:** find the detail that names or implies the reader (an age band, *students*,
   *parents*, the register of the language).
3. **Technique:** for each feature asked about, say what it is, then how it persuades:
   * a rhetorical question involves the reader and makes them think of their own wish;
   * a testimonial from someone like the reader makes the claim believable;
   * imperatives (*Sign up*, *Join us*) push the reader to act;
   * pictures of people like the reader help them imagine taking part.

## 3. Literal and inference questions: POINT AND PROOF

1. Underline the question word and the marks. Two marks usually means two ideas.
2. Find the lines first. For literal questions, the answer is stated; keep it short.
3. For inference, make the **point** (what you work out), then give the **proof** (the
   detail in the text that shows it). *He was sad; the spanner was already spotless, so the
   wiping was only to keep his hands busy.*
4. Answer in your own words unless the question asks for a quotation or a word.

## 4. Vocabulary in context: SWAP TEST

1. Read the whole sentence and the one before it.
2. Decide what the word must mean **here** (a word can mean different things elsewhere).
3. **Swap** your meaning or your chosen word into the sentence. If the sentence keeps its
   meaning, it is right.
4. For "find a word that means …", check the word class: a noun for a noun, an adverb for
   an adverb (*awkwardly*, not *awkward*).

## 5. Language for effect: QUOTE, MEANING, EFFECT

1. **Quote** the exact word or phrase.
2. **Meaning:** what it suggests literally and by association (*a sigh* is a sound of
   sadness or tiredness; *crushed* is violent and quick).
3. **Effect:** what it makes the reader feel or understand about the character, place or
   idea, and why that fits the moment in the text.

Naming the device (simile, personification) earns nothing on its own; the effect earns
the marks.

## 6. Summary: FIND, STRIP, REWRITE, JOIN

1. **Find** the paragraphs the question names and underline every point that answers it.
2. **Strip** examples, statistics and repeated ideas: keep the general point (*boredom can
   make us more creative*, not *people who did a dull task*).
3. **Rewrite** each point in your own words and structures; keep technical words that have
   no simple substitute.
4. **Join** the points in continuous prose with linking words (*also, in addition,
   finally, which*), after the given opening words.
5. Count the words (the opening is not counted) and stay within the limit.

## 7. The final check

1. Editing: one correction per error, grammar only.
2. Every question answered; two-mark answers have two ideas.
3. Language-for-effect answers have a quote, a meaning and an effect.
4. Summary: points, not examples; own words; within the word limit.
