# The P3 toolkit: model method, time line and the thinking routine

These are the *recommended* ways Singapore schools expect P3 pupils to solve
and present problems: the shapes the marker expects. When checking an answer
key or marking work, "correct" means this method, not just this number.

---

## 0. The thinking routine the schools teach: UnPAC + NTUC

**Un**derstand → **P**lan → **A**ct → **C**heck. The P3 version of Pólya.

| step | what the learner actually does | what you say |
|---|---|---|
| Understand | reads twice; underlines the question; circles comparison words (*fewer, more, times, each, altogether, left, at first*) | "What is the question asking? Point to it." |
| Plan | names the model (part-whole / comparison / before-after / time line); draws it | "Which picture is this? Draw it before any number." |
| Act | one number sentence per line, unit on each line | "One equation per line." |
| Check | **N-T-U-C**: Number transfer (copied correctly?), Units, Calculation (re-add), does the answer answer the question | "Do NTUC before you move on." |

For a learner who finds self-checking hard, the routine is a *physical*
checklist — four boxes drawn in the margin to tick. It moves the
self-monitoring out of the head and onto the page.

## 1. Part-whole model

Use when a whole is split into parts and one part is missing.

```
|------ Friday ------|---- Saturday ----|--- ? ---|
|<------------------- 1250 plates ------------------->|
```
468 + 395 = 863 (Friday and Saturday)
1250 − 863 = 387. **The learner sold 387 plates on Sunday.**

One number sentence per line, then a statement with the noun.

## 2. Comparison model (bars lined up on the left)

Use for *more than / fewer than / times as many*. Always draw the longer bar
first and line up the left edges.

### 2a. More / fewer (additive)
"Ben has 45 fewer stickers than Siti" ⇒ Siti's bar is longer: Siti = Ben + 45.
```
Ben   |------- 230 -------|
Siti  |------- 230 -------|-- 45 --|
```
*Trap:* "fewer than Siti" ⇒ Siti has more. Circle the name after the comparison word.

### 2b. Times as many (units)
Give the smallest amount 1 unit; "3 times as many" is 3 units. If one bar is
"1 unit and 12 more", take every extra away from the total first, then share
what is left equally into units.
```
Blue   |--u--|--u--|
Red    |--u--|
Green  |--u--|-- 12 --|
```
4 units + 12 = 92 → 4 units = 80 → 1 unit = **20** red.

### 2c. Sum and difference
Take the difference away from the sum and two equal bars are left:
*(sum − difference) ÷ 2 = the smaller number.* Teach this shape; it generalises
and the model shows why.

### 2d. Two purchases differing in one item
Line the two purchases up. Whatever is the same cancels, so the difference in
cost pays for the difference in items: 3 pens + 2 rulers = $11 and 3 pens +
5 rulers = $17 → 3 rulers = $6 → 1 ruler = **$2**.

## 3. Before–after model

Use when a quantity changes and the *new* relationship is given.

Draw the **after** bar first ("now she has 4 times as many as her brother"),
then undo the change to get the before: after = 4 × 25 = 100; she had bought
18, so before = 100 − 18 = **82**.

Equal at first, then one gains and the other loses: the gap between them is
the gain plus the loss. If afterwards one is 3 times the other, that gap is
2 units.

## 4. Time line (compulsory for duration questions)

Draw a horizontal line, mark the known times, jump **to the next whole hour
first**, then the rest. Never subtract times as decimals.

```
3.40 p.m. --20 min--> 4.00 p.m. --2 h--> 6.00 p.m. --15 min--> 6.15 p.m.
```
Duration = 20 min + 2 h + 15 min = **2 h 35 min**.

Going backwards works the same way: from the end time, jump back to the
o'clock, then the rest. Across midnight, always break the jump at 00 00.

## 5. Equal-groups and best-buy reasoning

Compare the price per item first. Take as many full bundles as you need
without going over, then top up with singles; buying extra items is not
"least" unless the question allows it. With "buy n, get m free", count the
items each price really buys (e.g. 6 + 2 free = 8 per bundle) before scaling.

## 6. Fractions — the two moves

1. **Same denominator first.** Related denominators: multiply top and bottom
   of the smaller-denominator fraction by the same number.
   2/4 + 3/8 → 4/8 + 3/8 = 7/8 · 1/4 + 5/12 → 3/12 + 5/12 = 8/12 = **2/3**
2. **Simplest form always.** Divide top and bottom by the same number until
   nothing else divides both.

Comparing unlike fractions without a calculator: benchmark. 1/5 is "a small
slice", 3/7 is "a bit less than half" (3.5/7 would be half), 9/11 is "nearly
all". So 1/5 < 3/7 < 9/11. Or make equivalents with denominator 10 when 5 and
10 are involved: 3/5 = 6/10 < 7/10, and 1/7 < 1/5 because sevenths are
smaller pieces.

## 7. Area and perimeter — the fence and the carpet

Perimeter = the **fence** around (add every side, cm). Area = the **carpet**
inside (length × breadth, cm²). Two figures can have the same fence and
different carpets: 6 × 6 has area 36 cm² and perimeter 24 cm; 3 × 9 has area
27 cm² and perimeter 24 cm.

**Wire problems.** The wire *is* the perimeter: bend it into a new shape and
the perimeter stays the same (rectangle → perimeter → square side → area).

**Shapes made of identical rectangles.** Label *every* outside edge first,
using the rectangles' length and breadth, then add.

**Counting squares.** Whole squares count 1, half-squares (diagonal cut) count ½.

## 8. Angles and lines — hand tools

- **Right angle test:** the corner of a sheet of paper. Fits exactly → right
  angle. Gap left → acute. Paper corner hides inside → obtuse.
- "Inside the figure" means *every* corner, marked or not — including the
  unmarked corners of a notched shape. Keys often undercount these.
- **Parallel on a grid:** count the steps. AB goes 2 across and 4 down; any
  line going 2 across and 4 down (or 1 across 2 down) is parallel. Draw it
  through the given point by repeating the same steps.
- **Perpendicular on a grid:** swap the two step counts and flip one
  direction. A line going 2 across, 4 down is perpendicular to one going
  4 across, 2 up. A line through P perpendicular to a "1 across, 1 down" line
  is "1 across, 1 up". Check with the paper corner.
