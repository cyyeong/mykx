# Topic playbooks: must-know, hooks, recipes, traps

One section per examinable topic. Each has: **Must-know** (the facts to memorise), **Hook** (how to remember),
**Recipe** (the steps to write), **Traps** (what learners commonly get wrong).
Read only the sections you need for today's target.

---
## 1. Whole Numbers (≈13 marks/paper, mostly Paper 1)

**Must-know**
- Place value to millions; reading numerals ("two hundred and fifty-six thousand and thirty" = 256 030 — the *and* means a gap of zeros).
- Rounding: look ONE place to the right of the target place. 5 or more → round up. *Nearest hundred:* 15 745 → look at tens digit 4 → **15 700**. *Nearest thousand → 56 000* means the real number lies from 55 500 up to 56 499.
- Factors & multiples: common factors of 12 and 21 → {1, 3}; first common multiple = LCM (6 and 8 → 24); "second common multiple" = 2 × LCM.
- Order of operations: **Brackets → Of/Orders → Divide/Multiply (left to right) → Add/Subtract (left to right)**. 64 − (9 + 7) ÷ 4 × 2 = 64 − 16 ÷ 4 × 2 = 64 − 4 × 2 = 64 − 8 = **56**.
- Divisibility: 2 (even), 3 (digit sum), 4 (last two digits), 5 (ends 0/5), 9 (digit sum), 10.
- Patterns: see heuristics §11.

**Hooks**
- Rounding: *"Five or more, raise the score; four or less, let it rest."* Underline the target digit, circle the one to its right.
- Rounding to nearest 1000 range: *"Half a thousand either side"* → 56 000 covers 55 500–56 499.
- BODMAS as a staircase: brackets at the top, ± at the bottom; you may only step down, never up.

**Recipe for "which number rounds to…"**: write the range first (lower bound = target − half unit, upper bound = target + half unit − 1), then test each option.

**Traps**
- Rounding 8.755 to the nearest hundredth: the hundredths digit is 5, next digit 5 → **8.76** (not 8.75, not 8.8).
- "Round to nearest hundred" ≠ "nearest hundredth".
- Forgetting left-to-right for ÷ and ×.
- Number patterns: counting the *position* of the 17th term vs the *value*.

## 2. Fractions (≈14 marks/paper — the biggest topic)

**Must-know**
- Mixed ↔ improper: $5\frac{3}{4} = \frac{23}{4}$ (5 × 4 + 3). "How many quarters in $4\frac{1}{2}$?" → 18.
- Add/subtract: common denominator. $6\frac{1}{8} − 2\frac{3}{4} = 6\frac{1}{8} − 2\frac{6}{8} = 3\frac{3}{8}$.
- Multiply: cancel first, then top × top, bottom × bottom. "of" means ×.
- Divide: **Keep, Change, Flip** — keep the first, change ÷ to ×, flip the second. $\frac{2}{3} ÷ \frac{9}{10} = \frac{2}{3} × \frac{10}{9} = \frac{20}{27}$. $\frac{4}{9} ÷ 12 = \frac{4}{9} × \frac{1}{12} = \frac{1}{27}$.
- Whole ÷ fraction: $4 ÷ \frac{2}{5} = 4 × \frac{5}{2} = 10$. "How many $\frac{7}{50}$ m pieces in 5 m?" → $5 ÷ \frac{7}{50} = \frac{250}{7} = 35$ r $\frac{5}{7}$ of a piece → leftover $\frac{5}{7} × \frac{7}{50} = \frac{1}{10}$ m.
- Compare: same denominator or compare to $\frac{1}{2}$; a fraction between $\frac{1}{3}$ and $\frac{2}{3}$ → $\frac{1}{2}$ (or make sixths: between $\frac{2}{6}$ and $\frac{4}{6}$ → $\frac{3}{6}$).
- Fraction ↔ decimal: make the denominator 10/100/1000 ($\frac{9}{20} = \frac{45}{100} = 0.45$), or memorise $\frac{1}{8} = 0.125$, $\frac{3}{8} = 0.375$, $\frac{5}{8} = 0.625$, $\frac{7}{8} = 0.875$.

**Hooks**
- Keep-Change-Flip: *"Keep, Change, Flip (order doesn't matter, the food does)."*
- "Of the remainder" → draw a *new* bar for the remainder (heuristics §4).
- Fraction of a figure: count equal pieces; if pieces aren't equal, cut them until they are (triangles into halves).

**Recipe for fraction word problems**: (1) whole = how many units? (choose LCM of all denominators mentioned) (2) label each cut (3) find the unit from the one number given (4) answer what was asked.

**Traps**
- Adding denominators. Forgetting to convert the whole number when dividing.
- "$\frac{1}{4}$ of the remainder" treated as $\frac{1}{4}$ of the whole.
- Leaving an answer as an improper fraction when "mixed number in simplest form" is asked.
- Cancelling across an addition.

## 3. Decimals (≈4 marks/paper, all short)

**Must-know**: ×10/100/1000 moves the point right; ÷ moves it left (70 ÷ 3500 = 0.02). Reading scales: count divisions → (end − start) ÷ divisions = one step (4 to 6 in 10 steps → 0.2 each). Ordering: pad with zeros (0.8, 0.88, 0.808 → 0.800, 0.880, 0.808). Decimal → mixed number: 5.08 = $5\frac{8}{100} = 5\frac{2}{25}$; 2.3 = $2\frac{3}{10}$.
**Hook**: "Point walks toward the bigger number when you multiply."
**Trap**: 0.35 × 90 — do 35 × 90 = 3150 then two decimal places → 31.5.

## 4. Percentage (≈7 marks/paper)

**Must-know**
- % → fraction (memorise: 10% = $\frac{1}{10}$, 20% = $\frac{1}{5}$, 25% = $\frac{1}{4}$, 40% = $\frac{2}{5}$, 50% = $\frac{1}{2}$, 60% = $\frac{3}{5}$, 75% = $\frac{3}{4}$, 12.5% = $\frac{1}{8}$).
- % of a quantity: find 1% (or 10%) first. 52% of 150 → 10% = 15 → 50% = 75, 2% = 3 → 78.
- % change = change ÷ original × 100. Increase 28 → 35: 7 ÷ 28 = 25%.
- Discount: pay (100 − d)%. GST/interest: pay (100 + r)%. Find original from final: final ÷ its %.
- "Percentage of the remainder" is a remainder-concept question (heuristics §4): 25% books, then 20% of remaining → 20% × 75% = 15% → saved 60%.
- Successive ±50% is NOT back to the start: ×1.5 × 0.5 = 0.75.

**Hooks**: "% change — divide by the OLD"; "discount: what you pay is 100 minus."
**Recipe for 'find the original'**: write "final = ___% of original" → original = final ÷ that %.
**Traps**: dividing by the new value in % change; treating $ discount and % discount alike.

## 5. Ratio (≈4 marks/paper directly; the *method* worth 22)

**Must-know**: same units before writing a ratio (550 mL : 1 L = 11 : 20). Ratio ↔ fraction ↔ %. Equivalent ratios by × or ÷. Three-way ratio via repeated identity (heuristics §3). Changing ratios → Before-After (heuristics §2). Ratio from a bar graph = the counts, simplified. "Greatest number of cakes" with a ratio and stock (butter : sugar 3 : 2, 150 g butter per cake → 100 g sugar per cake; with 1 kg butter and 500 g sugar: 1000 ÷ 150 = 6 r 100, 500 ÷ 100 = 5 → **5**, limited by sugar).
**Hooks**: "Ratio = recipe: same units, simplest form"; "Shared friend gets the same size" (repeated identity).
**Traps**: writing the ratio in the wrong order; simplifying only one side; forgetting to convert units.

## 6. Algebra (≈5 marks/paper)

**Must-know**: form an expression from words (6 cans per carton, w cartons → 6w; "$2 less than a plate" → plate − 2; $20 − (plate + bowl) = a). Simplify by collecting like terms: 16n + 7 − 4n + 11 = 12n + 18. Evaluate by substituting with brackets: 17 + 2(3) = 23; $\frac{3x}{4} + \frac{32}{x}$ at x = 8 → 6 + 4 = 10. Solve one-step/two-step: 10y + 2 = 72 → y = 7.
**Hooks**: "Like terms are the same letter — apples with apples"; "Substitute = swap the letter for the number **in brackets**".
**Traps**: 2y when y = 3 is 6, not 23; sign errors when subtracting; dividing $\frac{6m − 2}{3}$ → $2m − \frac{2}{3}$ (divide *both* terms).

## 7. Rate & charges tables (≈4 marks/paper)

**Must-know**: unit price (1.6 kg at $2.40 per 100 g → 16 × 2.40 = $38.40); best buy (compare price per item, then use leftover money for singles); "every additional 30 min **or part thereof**" → round the leftover blocks UP; work rate (9 booklets per 2 min → 40 min gives 180); flow rate (litres per minute → height change per minute = rate ÷ base area).
**Hook**: "Part thereof = the shop rounds UP, never down."
**Recipe**: total time → minus first block → leftover → ÷ block length → round up → × rate → + first-block charge. Reverse for "find the first-hour charge".

## 8. Average (≈2 marks/paper, but in every paper)

**Must-know**: Average = total ÷ number, so **total = average × number**. Missing item = total − sum of the rest. Combined average = combined total ÷ combined count. "Largest possible difference" with averages → make one number as large as allowed (999 for a 3-digit) and the other = remaining total − that.
**Hook**: "Average? Go to TOTAL first."
**Trap**: averaging averages; forgetting the count changes when a member leaves.

## 9. Measurement & Time (≈3 marks/paper, all Paper 1)

**Must-know**: 1 km = 1000 m, 1 m = 100 cm, 1 kg = 1000 g, 1 L = 1000 mL = 1000 cm³, 1 h = 60 min. 3465 cm = 34 m 65 cm. 8 h 45 min = 525 min. 24-hour clock: 10 pm = 22 00; 2 h before = 20 00. Duration: jump to the next hour, then count (11 50 → 12 00 is 10 min, then 30 → 40 min). Estimation: a 50-cent coin ≈ 7 g, so ten ≈ 70 g; an alarm clock ≈ 0.2 kg; a 30-cm ruler ≈ 20 g.
**Hook**: "King Henry Died Drinking Chocolate Milk" (k, h, da, base, d, c, m) — or simply "bigger unit → smaller number".
**Traps**: 3 kg 70 g = 3.07 kg (not 3.7); 2¼ L = 2250 mL; mixing up 12-h and 24-h.

## 10. Area & Perimeter (≈4 marks/paper)

**Must-know**: rectangle A = l × b, P = 2(l + b); square A = s², so s = √A (area 289 → side 17); triangle A = ½ × base × height (height must be **perpendicular** to the chosen base — use the dotted height even if it falls outside the triangle). Composite: cut into rectangles, or big minus small. Perimeter of an L-shape = perimeter of the enclosing rectangle. On a grid, count unit edges for perimeter, unit squares for area.
**Hooks**: "Triangle = half the rectangle around it"; "Height is where the right-angle mark is."
**Recipe for shaded triangles inside squares/rectangles**: choose a base that lies on a straight line you know the length of, then the height is the perpendicular distance to the opposite vertex. one school (squares 14 and 6, big square's side split 8 + 6): triangle AFH has vertical base FH = 14 − 6 = 8 cm and its height is the horizontal distance from A to that line = 8 cm → ½ × 8 × 8 = **32 cm²**.

## 11. Circles (≈7 marks/paper, Paper 2, 4–5 marks each)

**Must-know**: circumference = πd = 2πr; area = πr². Semicircle arc = ½πd; quadrant arc = ¼ × 2πr = ½πr. Quadrant area = ¼πr². Use the π the question gives (3.14 or $\frac{22}{7}$; $\frac{22}{7}$ with radius 7 or 14 gives whole numbers).
- **Perimeter of a shaded part = only the lines that touch the shaded region** — include straight edges! Trace with a finger and list each piece.
- Shaded area tricks: (square − quadrant) is the common "corner" piece; two such corners = 2(r² − ¼πr²); a leaf/lens = 2 × (quadrant − triangle).
- Rolling wheel: distance = turns × circumference.
**Hooks**: "C = πd — 'Cherry pie is delicious'; A = πr² — 'Apple pies are too'." "Perimeter = walk around the shaded shape and count every road you step on."
**Recipe**: (1) write r (not d!) (2) list every boundary piece with its formula (3) compute each (4) add. For area: name each region "quadrant", "square", "triangle" before calculating.
**Traps**: using d where r is needed; forgetting straight sides in a perimeter; rounding π-values mid-way (keep full digits, round at the end only if asked).

## 12. Volume (≈7 marks/paper, Paper 2, 4–5 marks each)

**Must-know**: V = l × b × h; 1 L = 1000 cm³; cube edge = ∛V (27 → 3). Height of water = V ÷ base area. **Pouring between tanks keeps total volume fixed**: equal heights → h = total volume ÷ (sum of base areas). "Height in Y is twice height in X" → 75h + 50(2h) = 1365. Filling/draining at a rate → height changes at (rate ÷ base area) cm per minute. Packing cuboids: divide along each dimension separately (70 ÷ 5 = 14, 24 ÷ 4 = 6, 50 ÷ 6 = 8 r 2 → then use the leftover slab if the small box can turn).
**Hooks**: "Water can't disappear — total volume is your anchor"; "Height = Volume ÷ Floor".
**Recipe (two-tank pour)**: (1) total water (2) sum of base areas (3) h = total ÷ sum (4) answer the actual question (volume poured, height, time).
**Traps**: mixing L and cm³; using the tank height instead of the water height; forgetting one of the 6 painted faces / 12 edges of a cube (wire around a cube edge 4 cm → 12 × 4 = 48).

## 13. Angles & Geometry (≈13 marks/paper — high yield and rule-based)

**The seven facts (memorise as a list of 7):**
1. Angles on a straight line = 180°.
2. Angles at a point = 360°.
3. Vertically opposite angles are equal.
4. Angle sum of a triangle = 180°; equilateral → all 60°; isosceles → two equal base angles opposite the two equal sides; right-angled → other two sum to 90°.
5. Square/rectangle: all 90°, diagonals of a square make 45°.
6. Parallelogram & rhombus: opposite angles equal; angles between parallel sides add to 180°; rhombus diagonals bisect the angles (each half).
7. Trapezium: one pair of parallel sides → the two angles between them add to 180°.
Plus: parallel lines → alternate (Z) and corresponding (F) angles are equal; a fold makes mirror-image equal angles; "is / is not a parallelogram" → check both pairs of opposite sides parallel (angle sums 180°).

**Hooks**: "Straight = 180, Point = 360, Triangle = 180 — the *180-360-180 chant*." Isosceles: "the equal angles sit *opposite* the equal sides (the ticks)." Rhombus: "a squashed square — diagonals cut every corner in half."

**Recipe**: (1) mark every given angle and every equal-side tick on the figure (2) write the name of the shape and which facts apply (3) find angles one at a time, writing the reason next to each (e.g. "∠EBD = (180 − 66) ÷ 2 = 57° (isos. triangle)") (4) stop when you reach the asked angle. Reasons earn method marks.

**Traps**: assuming a triangle is isosceles without ticks; using 360 for a triangle; reading the wrong angle when three letters are given (the middle letter is the vertex); forgetting that a rhombus diagonal creates two isosceles triangles.

## 14. Nets, solids, unit cubes, drawing on grids (≈3 marks/paper)

**Must-know**: a cube net has 6 squares, never 4 in a row with 2 on the same side, never a 2×2 block. Square-pyramid net: 1 square + 4 triangles each attached to a different side of the square (or chained correctly). Cuboid net: 2 identical squares + 4 identical rectangles → perimeter/length questions use the net's edge lengths. Unit cubes: count per column; "least cubes to complete a big cube" = n³ − current. Views: top/front/side — "max cubes to add without changing two views" → fill columns up to the height the views allow. Drawing: on a grid, a rectangle with the same area as a square of side 4 → 2 × 8; a square with the same perimeter as a parallelogram → measure sides, ÷ 4.
**Hook**: "A net folds without any face overlapping — trace it in your head, face by face."

## 15. Data (≈10 marks/paper)

**Must-know**: read scales carefully (each division = ?); bar graph → counts, ratios, "greatest increase" (difference between consecutive bars/points); line graph → change over an interval (steepest = greatest change; flat = no change; rate = change ÷ time); pie chart → fractions/percentages of a whole (a right-angle sector = ¼ = 25%; a half = 50%); **table + graph combos** → use the % table to identify unlabeled bars (biggest % = tallest bar).
- **True / False / Not possible to tell**: True/False only if it can be computed from the given numbers; "Not possible" when a needed total or split is missing (e.g. Stall A's total not given → can't compare counts with Stall B). "Same number of mangoes and pears" when 55% of 300 = 165 must split into two *whole* equal numbers → 82.5 impossible → **False**.
**Hooks**: "Pie = part of ONE whole; two pies can't be compared unless you know both totals." "Steepest line = fastest change."
**Traps**: comparing percentages across two pies with different totals; misreading a bar between gridlines; forgetting 'each division' isn't always 1.
