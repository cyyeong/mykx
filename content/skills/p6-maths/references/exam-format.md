# PSLE Mathematics: format, scope, time budgeting

## Dates (SEAB 2026 timetable)

The national timetable (SEAB) sets the two paper dates; the learner's own exam dates are in their profile. Both papers are on the same morning with a break between.

## Format (revised for 2026 — first cohort fully on the 2021 syllabus)

| Paper | Booklet | Item type | Qns | Marks each | Total | Calculator |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | Multiple choice | 10 | 1 | 10 | No |
| 1 | A | Multiple choice | 8 | 2 | 16 | No |
| 1 | B | Short answer | 12 | 2 | 24 | No |
| 2 | — | Short answer | 5 | 2 | 10 | Yes |
| 2 | — | Structured / long answer | 10 | 3, 4 or 5 | 40 | Yes |
| | | | **45** | | **100** | |

Paper 1 = 50 marks (30 questions), Paper 2 = 50 marks (15 questions). Equal weight — Paper 1 can no longer be "rushed".

**What changed from 2025:** Booklet A went from 15 to 18 MCQ (8 two-mark MCQs instead of 5); Booklet B lost its five 1-mark questions — all 12 are now 2 marks; Paper 2 dropped from 12 to 10 structured questions. Paper 1 is 10 minutes longer, Paper 2 10 minutes shorter.

**Practical consequence — "the 2-mark trap":** every Booklet B and Paper 2 short-answer question is worth 2 marks, so a careless slip with no working loses both. Write one line of working even when the answer is obvious; a correct method with a wrong final answer usually still earns 1 mark.

## Syllabus scope (MOE 2021 Primary Mathematics syllabus, examinable P1–P6 content)
The 2021 syllabus document (updated Oct 2025) applies to Primary 6 from 2026, so this is the first PSLE cohort fully on it. Statement by statement, with levels, see `syllabus-map.md`.
**Removed — do not spend time on:** Speed (moved to Secondary 1), 8-point compass directions.
**Examinable topics:** Whole numbers (incl. factors/multiples, order of operations, number patterns) · Fractions · Decimals · Percentage · Ratio (now taught in P6) · Average (now P6) · Rate · Algebra (expressions in one variable) · Measurement (length, mass, volume/capacity, time, money) · Area & perimeter (incl. composite figures, triangles) · Circles (circumference, area, semicircle, quarter circle, composite) · Volume of cube/cuboid (incl. liquids, ℓ ↔ cm³) · Angles (straight line, point, triangle, square/rectangle, parallelogram, rhombus, trapezium, parallel & perpendicular lines, symmetry) · Nets of cubes, cuboids, prisms, pyramids and 2D representations of solids (P4) · Solids built from unit cubes; cubes and cuboids drawn on an isometric grid (P5) · Data: tables, bar graphs, line graphs, pie charts, incl. "True / False / Not possible to tell" statements.

Assessment objectives: AO1 recall & compute · AO2 interpret and apply in context · AO3 reason, infer, choose a strategy. Roughly, Booklet A and the short-answer sections are AO1/AO2; the 4–5 mark questions are AO2/AO3.

## Time budgeting to teach

**Paper 1 (70 min for 30 questions):**
- Booklet A (18 MCQ): aim for 25 min. 1-mark MCQ ≈ 1 min each; 2-mark MCQ ≈ 2 min. If stuck for 2 min, circle it, shade a best guess, move on — never leave an MCQ blank.
- Booklet B (12 × 2 marks): aim for 35 min (≈ 3 min each). Leaves ~10 min to check.
- No calculator: estimate first ("about 30?"), then compute; check by the inverse operation.

**Paper 2 (80 min for 15 questions, 50 marks):** ≈ 1.5 min per mark. Q1–5 (10 marks) in ~12 min; Q6–15 (40 marks) in ~60 min; ~8 min to check units and answer lines.
- Use the calculator for every multiplication/division — but write the expression first, so a method mark survives a key-press slip.
- Units in the answer line; angles need the ° symbol; money needs 2 decimal places.

## Marking realities worth telling the learner
- Method marks exist: a correct model/first step earns credit even if the final answer is wrong. Never leave a 4–5 mark question blank — draw the model, write the first equation.
- There is no penalty for a wrong MCQ answer — always shade something.
- Diagrams are "not drawn to scale" — never measure them (except questions that say "measure").
