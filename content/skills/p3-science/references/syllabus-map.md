# P3 Science syllabus map

The P3 end-of-year exam covers **exactly these seven sub-topics and nothing else**.
Every lesson, question and paper must sit inside them. Teaching outside the scope
spends a learner's limited attention on marks that do not exist.

| Theme | Sub-topic | Topic tag | Share of marks* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diversity | Diversity of living and non-living things | `diversity.living` | 9 % |
| Diversity | Classification of living things | `diversity.classification` | 20 % |
| Diversity | Diversity of materials | `diversity.materials` | 21 % |
| Cycles | Life cycle of plants | `cycles.plant` | 7 % |
| Cycles | Life cycle of animals | `cycles.animal` | 16 % |
| Interactions | Properties of magnets | `interactions.magnets` | 19 % |
| Interactions | Making and using magnets | `interactions.making_magnets` | 7 % |

\*Tallied from five recent school end-of-year papers (360 marks), kept as private reference. Diversity ≈ 50 %,
Cycles ≈ 23 %, Interactions ≈ 27 %. Questions are about half recall/understanding
(AO1) and half application and process skills (AO2), so **every topic is also
examined through experiments, tables, graphs and charts**.

## Official learning outcomes (MOE 2023) and the sub-skill tags

Use these tags exactly; they are the `subskills` in `skill.json`. Untagged or
misspelt tags make the statistics useless.

### 1. Diversity of living and non-living things — `diversity.living`
Outcome: describe the characteristics of living things — **need water, food and
air to survive; grow; respond (to changes); reproduce**. Observe living and
non-living things and infer differences.

| Tag | Covers |
|---|---|
| `characteristics` | grow / respond to changes / reproduce / need air, food, water — naming which one a scenario shows |
| `needs` | why something died or survived (no air holes, no water, no food) |
| `lnl_identify` | deciding living vs non-living, including traps (fire, cloud, robot, car, seed, dead leaf) |
| `needs_experiment` | set-ups testing needs: choosing the two set-ups that differ in one condition |

### 2. Classification of living things — `diversity.classification`
Outcome: recognise broad groups — **plants** (flowering, non-flowering), **animals**
(amphibians, birds, fish, insects, mammals, reptiles), **fungi** (mould, mushroom,
yeast), **bacteria** — and classify them by common observable characteristics.

| Tag | Covers |
|---|---|
| `plant_fungi_groups` | flowering vs non-flowering plants (seeds vs spores, flowers/fruits); fungi are not plants |
| `fungi_bacteria` | what fungi/bacteria are like, useful vs harmful, what mould needs to grow (water, warmth, food, air) |
| `animal_groups` | the six animal groups and their tell-tale features |
| `insect_or_not` | 3 body parts, 6 legs, 1 pair of feelers; spider / centipede traps |
| `chart_heading` | reading classification charts, flowcharts, tick tables; inferring a missing heading |

### 3. Diversity of materials — `diversity.materials`
Outcome: relate the use of **ceramic, fabric, glass, metal, plastic, rubber, wood**
to their physical properties; compare materials by **strength, flexibility,
waterproof, transparency (ability to let light pass through), ability to float or
sink**; use data to justify choices.

| Tag | Covers |
|---|---|
| `material_types` | naming the seven materials and typical objects made of each |
| `strength` | ability to hold a load without breaking (brick/weight tests) |
| `flexibility` | ability to bend without breaking (bending tests, distance bent) |
| `waterproof` | does not allow water to pass through (bowl/cloth over container tests) |
| `absorbency` | how much water a material soaks up (strips in water, water left, mass gained) |
| `transparency` | how much light passes through (torch + light sensor, seeing an object clearly / faintly / not at all) |
| `float_sink` | tank diagrams, choosing a material for a boat/float/anchor |
| `property_to_use` | "most suitable material for X — explain" |
| `fair_test` | changed / measured / kept-the-same variables in materials experiments |

### 4. Life cycle of plants — `cycles.plant`
Outcome: show that different living things have different life cycles; observe
and compare life cycles of plants grown from seeds.

| Tag | Covers |
|---|---|
| `plant_stages` | seed → germinating seed → young plant (seedling) → adult plant (flowers, fruits with seeds) → seed |
| `germination` | needs **water, air, warmth** (light is NOT needed); root grows first; choosing set-ups to test a condition |
| `plant_cycle_reasoning` | why keeping seeds gives a continuous supply; young plant can make its own food once it has leaves; life cycle repeats |

### 5. Life cycle of animals — `cycles.animal`
Outcome: observe and compare the life cycles of **butterfly, beetle, mosquito,
grasshopper, cockroach, chicken, frog** (these seven are named in the syllabus).

| Tag | Covers |
|---|---|
| `three_stage` | grasshopper, cockroach (egg → nymph → adult), chicken (egg → chick → adult), frog (egg → tadpole → adult) |
| `four_stage` | butterfly, beetle, mosquito (egg → larva → pupa → adult) |
| `cycle_diagram` | drawing/choosing a correctly arrowed cycle; no first or last stage |
| `stage_features` | which stage feeds, moults, lives in water, looks like the adult |
| `duration_graph` | reading days/weeks per stage; "after hatching"; "on day N which stage" |
| `mosquito_control` | breaking the mosquito life cycle (stagnant water, oil layer, fish, Wolbachia) |

### 6. Properties of magnets — `interactions.magnets`
Outcome: a magnet can exert a push or a pull; magnets are made of iron or steel;
have two poles; a freely suspended bar magnet rests pointing north–south; unlike
poles attract and like poles repel; magnets attract magnetic materials; compare
magnets, non-magnets and magnetic materials.

| Tag | Covers |
|---|---|
| `poles` | two poles; strongest at the poles (clips cluster at the ends; a ring/button magnet's poles are its flat faces) |
| `attract_repel` | like repel, unlike attract; floating rings; which way magnets move |
| `pole_puzzle` | chains of touching magnets — work out unlabelled poles |
| `magnetic_materials` | iron, steel, nickel, cobalt vs non-magnetic (incl. aluminium, copper) |
| `magnet_vs_magnetic` | attraction alone does not prove a magnet; only a magnet can be repelled |
| `north_south` | freely suspended magnet / compass needle direction |
| `strength_test` | clips attracted, distance of attraction, sheets held; fair-test variables |

### 7. Making and using magnets — `interactions.making_magnets`
Outcome: make a magnet by the **stroke method** and the **electrical method**;
recognise uses of magnets in everyday objects.

| Tag | Covers |
|---|---|
| `stroke_method` | one pole, one direction, lift away; more strokes → stronger; back-and-forth fails |
| `electrical_method` | coil of wire round an iron/steel core + battery; more turns / more batteries → stronger; switch on/off |
| `makeable_materials` | only magnetic materials (iron, steel) can be made into magnets — copper, aluminium, wood cannot |
| `uses` | doorstop, fridge/cabinet catches, compass, recycling separator, scrapyard crane, maglev, toys, notice-board magnets |

## Depth limits — what NOT to teach

These are not in the P3 exam. If the learner asks out of curiosity, answer in one or two
fun sentences and say "that's a P4/P5 thing — not in your exam".

- Plant **parts and their functions** in depth (transport of water, photosynthesis
  mechanics, pollination, seed dispersal). P3 only needs to *name* root, stem, leaf,
  flower, fruit, seed when they appear in a life cycle or classification question.
- Human digestive system, states of matter, light and shadows / reflection, heat,
  water cycle, cells, electrical circuits (beyond "batteries + coil = electromagnet"),
  forces other than magnetic, food chains, adaptations, reproduction in detail.
- Light as a topic (shadows, reflection) is P4. "Lets light pass through" as a
  **property of materials** IS in scope.
- Fern/moss life cycles in detail; non-flowering seed plants (pine, cycad) are an
  exception mentioned in some schools — only raise them if the learner's school notes do.

**Stretch points sometimes seen in school papers** (teach briefly, after the core is
secure): the end of a nail where stroking stops becomes the pole *opposite* to the
stroking pole; magnets can weaken if dropped, hammered or heated; magnetic force
passes through non-magnetic materials (paper, plastic, water) — e.g. a magnet
holding a worksheet on a whiteboard.

## Exam format (school-set; no national exam at P3)

There is no national exam at P3; each school sets its own end-of-year paper. The common formats:

| Format | Booklet A (MCQ, 2 marks each) | Booklet B (open-ended) | Total | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Most common | 24 Q = 48 | 9–10 Q = 32 | 80 | 1 h 30 |
| Variant | 22 Q = 44 | 9 Q = 36 | 80 | 1 h 30 |
| Shorter | 18 Q = 36 | 8 Q = 24 | 60 | 1 h 10 – 1 h 15 |

- Default to the most common format (24 MCQ + Booklet B = 80 marks, 1 h 30) unless the learner's profile says otherwise.
- MCQs have four options **(1)–(4)** and are shaded on an Optical Answer Sheet.
- Open-ended parts are worth 1–2 marks each, shown in brackets **[ ]**.
- Typical OEQ answers expected: a one-line fact, a fill-in table, a circled word, a drawn life cycle, or a 2-mark "Do you agree? Explain" in CER form.
