# Interactions: magnets

Magnets are ~27 % of the paper and the most "puzzle-like" topic — great for an
easily bored kid because it can be *played with*. Get a fridge magnet, two bar or
ring magnets, paper clips, an iron nail, a wire and a battery if possible.

---

## 1. Core properties (`poles`, `attract_repel`, `north_south`)

- A magnet can **push (repel) or pull (attract)** — without touching.
- Magnets are made of **iron or steel** (magnetic materials).
- Every magnet has **two poles**, north (N) and south (S). The magnetic force is
  **strongest at the poles** — clips gather at the **ends** of a bar magnet, fewest
  in the middle.
  - U/horseshoe magnet: poles at the two **arm ends**.
  - Ring and button magnets: poles are the two **flat faces**, so every point on
    the bottom face attracts the same number of clips.
- **Unlike poles attract, like poles repel.** Hook: *"Opposites attract, twins fight."*
- A **freely suspended** bar magnet comes to rest pointing **north–south** (its
  north pole points north). A **compass needle** is a tiny magnet, so it also points
  north–south — and swings toward a nearby magnet.

**Traps.** Bigger magnet ≠ stronger magnet (e.g. biggest held fewest
sheets). "A magnet becomes stronger / weaker after it rests north–south" ✗.

---

## 2. Magnet vs magnetic material vs non-magnetic (`magnetic_materials`, `magnet_vs_magnetic`)

| Kind | Brought near N pole | Brought near S pole |
|---|---|---|
| **Magnet** | attracted at one end, **repelled** at the other | the opposite |
| **Magnetic material** (iron, steel, nickel, cobalt) | attracted | attracted |
| **Non-magnetic** (wood, plastic, rubber, glass, paper, fabric, **aluminium, copper**, gold, silver) | nothing | nothing |

- **Not all metals are magnetic** — aluminium foil, copper wire, a 'gold' coin are
  not (e.g. "all metals can be made into magnets" ✗).
- **Only a magnet can be repelled by a magnet.** Repulsion proves "magnet";
  attraction alone could be a magnet OR a magnetic material.
- "Definitely true / definitely false / not possible to tell" tables:
  floating ring = definitely a magnet; touching/resting ring = not possible to tell.

**Hook.** "*I Never Steal Cookies*" — Iron, Nickel, Steel, Cobalt are magnetic.

**Ladder.**
- R1: "Name two magnetic materials."
- R2: J is attracted by both poles; K: nothing; L: attracted by N, repelled by S —
  classify J, K, L.
- R3: "Y was attracted to the N pole. Tom says Y is a magnet. Agree?" → No;
  magnetic materials are also attracted; only repulsion would prove it.

## 3. Pole puzzles (`pole_puzzle`, `attract_repel`)

**Rule:** wherever two magnets **attract and touch**, the touching ends are
**opposite** poles. Wherever one magnet **floats** above another, the facing poles
are the **same**. Each bar magnet's two ends are opposite.

**Method (do it with a pencil, every time):**
1. Start from the one labelled pole.
2. Write the other end of that magnet (flip N↔S).
3. Cross each join: touching & attracting → **flip**; floating gap → **same**.
4. Repeat until you reach the box asked for.

Floating rings: a gap means **repel**,
so both rings at the gap are magnets with like poles facing; touching rings may be
attracting OR just resting.

Maglev / floating toy train: the magnets on the train and track
face each other with **like poles** so the train is pushed up.

Moving magnets on a table: S facing N → attract → **both move
towards each other** once released.

U-magnet chains: label every end with ± in the order of
attraction, then test each option for a like-pole contact.

---

## 4. Comparing magnetic strength (`strength_test`)

Three ways, all "more = stronger":
- **number of clips/pins** attracted;
- **distance** from which a clip/nail is first attracted — further = stronger
---

## 5. Making a magnet — stroke method (`stroke_method`, `makeable_materials`)

1. Use a **magnetic** object (iron nail, steel needle). Copper, aluminium, wood
   **cannot** be made into magnets.
2. Stroke with **one pole** of a magnet, **in one direction only**.
3. **Lift the magnet away** at the end of each stroke and bring it back to the
   start through the air (the diagram shows a loop of dashed arrows going one way).
4. **More strokes → stronger** magnet.

**Trap:** a diagram with arrows going **back and
forth** along the nail is the wrong method — even with more strokes it makes a weak
magnet or none. Check three things: material, direction pattern, number of strokes.

Stretch: the end of the nail where each stroke **finishes** becomes the pole
**opposite** to the stroking pole.

**Try it.** Stroke a steel needle 10 times one way, count pins it lifts; 30 more
strokes, count again; then try back-and-forth on a fresh needle. The learner records a
table — instant fair-test practice.

---

## 6. Making a magnet — electrical method (`electrical_method`)

- Coil **insulated wire** around an **iron/steel** nail or rod and connect it to a
  **battery** — an **electromagnet**. It is a magnet only while the current flows,
  so it can be **switched on and off** (a scrapyard crane drops the scrap when
  switched off).
- **More turns (coils) of wire → stronger.** **More batteries → stronger.**
---

## 7. Uses of magnets (`uses`)

| Use | Property used |
|---|---|
| Magnetic doorstop / cabinet & fridge door catch | attracts a steel plate, holds door |
| Notes/worksheets on a whiteboard | attraction passes through paper |
| Compass | freely moving magnet points north–south |
| Recycling plant: separating steel cans / scraps from aluminium, rubber | only magnetic materials are attracted; others fall off |
| Scrapyard crane (electromagnet) | switch on to lift, off to drop |
| Floating (maglev) train, floating toy | like poles repel |
| Magnetic fishing game, toy car pushed by a magnet | attract / repel without touching |

Not magnet uses: soap dispenser, plastic water bottle.
