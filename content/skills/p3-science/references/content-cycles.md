# Cycles: life cycles of plants and animals

---

## 1. Big ideas (examined every year)

- A **life cycle** is a **repeated pattern of change** a living thing goes through:
  it is born/hatches/germinates, grows, becomes an adult and **reproduces** — and
  the cycle starts again. There is **no first or last stage** (e.g. "the egg comes first" → disagree).
- Different living things have **different** life cycles (different stages and
  different lengths of time). "All animals have the same number of stages" ✗.
- Draw a life cycle as a **closed loop with arrows** — the last stage points back
  to the first.

## 2. Life cycle of a flowering plant (`plant_stages`, `germination`, `plant_cycle_reasoning`)

**seed → germinating seed → young plant (seedling) → adult plant (flowers → fruits
with seeds) → seed**

- **Germination** needs **water, air (oxygen) and warmth**. Light is **not** needed
  (the seed has stored food). Hook: *"WAW — Water, Air, Warmth"* (some schools say
  WOW: Water, Oxygen, Warmth).
- The **root** grows out first (to take in water and hold the plant), then the shoot.
- A **young plant** can **make its own food** once it has green leaves. It is like the adult in that way; it is not yet ready to flower.
- An **adult** plant **bears flowers and fruits** — that is how you know it is an
  adult (e.g. "the plant bears fruit"). Fruits contain **seeds** that can
  grow into new plants → keeping some rice grains / seeds gives a continuous supply
**Ladder.**
- R1: "What three things does a seed need to germinate?"
- R2: Table of set-ups (air/water/light/temperature) — "Which two show if light is
  needed?" (only light differs).
- R3: "A farmer keeps some harvested rice. Explain how this gives him rice to sell
  in future." → rice grains are seeds; they grow into adult plants that bear fruits
  (rice) again.

**Try it.** Green beans on wet cotton wool in two cups — one in a dark cupboard,
one by the window. Both germinate → light not needed for germination (great
"whoa" moment). Measure height daily — instant data table for practice questions.

## 3. The seven animals named in the syllabus

| Animal | Stages | Young looks like adult? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Butterfly | egg → **larva** (caterpillar) → **pupa** (chrysalis) → adult | No | 4 stages. Eggs laid on leaves the larva eats. Larva eats a lot, **moults**. Pupa does not feed. Adult sips nectar. |
| Beetle (mealworm beetle) | egg → **larva** (mealworm) → **pupa** → adult | No | 4 stages. Mealworm eats oats/bran/wood; pupa does not feed. |
| Mosquito | egg → **larva** (wriggler) → **pupa** (tumbler) → adult | No | 4 stages. **Egg, larva, pupa live in water**; larva breathes through a tube at the water surface; pupa does not feed. Adult flies; females bite. |
| Grasshopper | egg → **nymph** → adult | Yes (smaller, wings not fully formed) | 3 stages. Nymph **moults**. |
| Cockroach | egg (in an egg case) → **nymph** → adult | Yes | 3 stages. |
| Chicken | egg → **chick** → adult (hen / rooster) | Yes (at P3) | 3 stages. Egg hatches after about 3 weeks. |
| Frog | egg (frogspawn, in water) → **tadpole** → adult frog | **No** | 3 stages (some books add "young frog/froglet"). Tadpole lives in water and has a tail; adult lives on land and in water. |

**Patterns to say out loud:**
- 4-stage (butterfly, beetle, mosquito): young **never** looks like the adult.
- 3-stage insects (grasshopper, cockroach) have **nymphs**, never larvae.
- The frog is the odd one: 3 stages but the young does **not** look like the adult.
- Everything in the list **lays eggs**; nothing in the list gives birth.

**Hooks.**
- *"Butterflies, Beetles and Mosquitoes Make Pupae"* — the 4-stage club (B-B-M).
- *"Cockroach and Grasshopper are Nymph Neighbours"* — the 3-stage insects.
- Pupa = **P**ause: no eating, (almost) no moving, big changes inside.

**Ladder.**
- R1: "Name the stage after the egg for a mosquito / grasshopper / chicken."
- R2: Pick the correctly arrowed cycle; group a cockroach and a beetle (both on land? both lay eggs?).
- R3: "Christy says the egg stage comes first. Do you agree?" → No; the stages are
  repeated, there is no first or last stage.

## 4. Stage features — reading clues (`stage_features`)

| Clue in the question | Stage |
|---|---|
| food/leaves **decrease** during the stage | larva or nymph (or adult for some) |
| food stays the **same** while young | **pupa** (does not feed) — or egg |
| adult butterfly + leaves stay the same | adult does not eat leaves (drinks nectar) |
| "shed dry skin" | larva/nymph **moulting** |
| harmful to vegetable garden | larva (caterpillar eats leaves) — not the pupa |
| lives in water with a breathing tube | mosquito larva |
| comma-shaped, in water, does not feed | mosquito pupa |
| eggs laid in cracks of wood / on leaves | so the larva has **food** as soon as it hatches |

## 5. Graphs and tables of stage lengths (`duration_graph`)

Three standard questions — teach the **words**, the maths is easy:

| Question wording | What to add |
|---|---|
| "after the egg **hatches**, how long to become an **adult**" | larva/nymph + pupa (NOT egg, NOT adult) |
| "how long is the whole life cycle" | all bars |
| "on day 14, which stage" | add bars from the start until you pass day 14 (Q = 8 → larva from day 8 to 20) |

Other graph questions: "spends the most time in which stage" (tallest bar);
"similarities" between two organisms' cycles (both 4 stages; both spend the least
time as eggs; young of both do not look like the adult —, "do not name
the stages"). Always check the **units** (days vs weeks) and read bar tops along
the grid line with a ruler.

Temperature tables: "as temperature increases, the length of the
life cycle **decreases**" — relationship sentence = *"As ___ increases, ___
increases/decreases."*

## 6. Mosquitoes in Singapore (`mosquito_control`)

Real-life hook the learner will recognise: **dengue** is spread by adult female *Aedes*
mosquitoes. To break the life cycle, stop the **water** stages:
- remove stagnant water; turn over pails, flowerpot plates; change vase water often
  (eggs/larvae/pupae need still water);
- a layer of **oil** on the water blocks the larva's **breathing tube** so it cannot
  breathe;
- fish in ponds eat larvae;
- **Project Wolbachia** (NEA): special male mosquitoes are released; eggs from their
  mating **do not hatch**, so there is no larva, pupa or adult.

Answer pattern: *"[action] so that [stage] cannot [survive/develop], so fewer adult
mosquitoes"* — always link to the **stage** and the **water**.
