# Diversity: living things, classification, materials

Each block: **facts** (in exam words) → **traps** → **hook** (memory aid) →
**ladder** (R1 recall, R2 apply, R3 explain — climb, stop at first failure) →
**try it at home** (2-minute hands-on, optional but gold for an easily bored kid).

---

## 1. Living and non-living things (`diversity.living`)

**Facts.** Living things **need air, food and water to survive**, **grow**,
**respond to changes** around them, and **reproduce**. A non-living thing does not
have all of these. Plants are living: they grow, respond (turn towards light,
mimosa leaves fold when touched), reproduce and need air and water (they make their
own food). A **seed** is living (it can grow into a plant).

Matching scenario → characteristic (this is half the MCQs):

| Scenario says… | Characteristic |
|---|---|
| got taller / heavier / bigger over months | living things **grow** |
| there are more of them later; laid eggs; seeds grew into new plants | living things **reproduce** |
| moved away when touched; closed its leaves; fanned himself on a hot day; pupils got smaller in bright light | living things **respond to changes** |
| died without water / air holes / food | living things **need water / air / food to survive** |

**Traps.** Fire (grows, moves, "needs air" — but cannot reproduce, no food/water),
cloud, robot, car, toy that moves, wind-up toy, tablet/computer — all non-living.
"Moving" is not on the list; plants are living but don't walk. Bread is non-living,
but mould growing on bread is living. A dead leaf is non-living now.

**Hook.** *"GRR + AFW"* — living things go **G**row, **R**espond, **R**eproduce
and need **A**ir, **F**ood, **W**ater. Ask the learner to growl "GRR!" — it sticks.

**Ladder.**
- R1: "Name two things all living things need." (any two of air, food, water)
- R2: "Brandon's 2 hamsters became 5 after two months. Which characteristic?" (reproduce)
- R3: "A snail in a jar with a lid without holes died. Explain." → CER: no air
  could enter; living things need air to survive.

**Needs experiments** (`needs_experiment`). Pick the two set-ups where **only the
condition being tested is different**.

**Try it.** "Living or not?" speed round with 10 household objects — the learner must
give a GRR+AFW reason for each. Bonus: watch a mimosa (touch-me-not) in a park.

## 2. Plants, fungi and bacteria (`plant_fungi_groups`, `fungi_bacteria`)

**Plants** make their own food (with sunlight).
- **Flowering plants** bear flowers; flowers become **fruits** with **seeds**;
  reproduce by seeds (e.g. balsam, banana, rice, water lily, mango, hibiscus).
  Seeing a fruit or flower → flowering plant.
- **Non-flowering plants** do not bear flowers; ferns and mosses reproduce by
  **spores** (e.g. bird's-nest fern, staghorn fern, moss). Some flowering plants
  live in water (water lily), so "grows in water" does not decide the group.

**Fungi** (mould, mushroom, yeast) — **not plants**: **cannot make their own food**;
they feed on what they grow on; reproduce by **spores**. Mould grows best with
**water (moisture), warmth, food and air** — so toasted/dry bread or bread with a
moisture-absorbing packet grows less mould. Useful: yeast makes bread dough rise;
mushrooms are food; fungi break down dead things. Harmful: mould spoils food;
fungus grows on damp toenails (keep feet dry, cut nails); some mushrooms are
poisonous.

**Bacteria** — so small they can **only be seen with a microscope**; different
shapes; some useful (making yoghurt, cheese; breaking down dead things), some
harmful (cause diseases, food poisoning, tooth decay). Not all bacteria are harmful.

**Traps.** "Fungi are plants" ✗. "Mushrooms make their own food" ✗. "Yeast
reproduces by seeds" ✗. "Bacteria are used to make bread rise" ✗ (that's yeast).
"Yeast can be seen with the naked eye" ✗ (single yeast cells need a microscope).
"Grows in water → non-flowering" ✗.

**Hook.** *"Spores = ferns and fungi, but only ferns cook"* — both spread spores,
only the fern makes its own food. Flowers → fruit → seeds: "no flower, no fruit".

**Ladder.**
- R1: "Name the three kinds of fungi in your syllabus." (mould, mushroom, yeast)
- R2: tick table (spores ✓ / makes food ✗) → which is a mushroom?
- R3: "Bread A had mould, toasted bread C had almost none. Explain." → toasting
  removed water; mould (fungi) needs water to grow.

**Try it.** Two slices of bread in sealed bags — one sprinkled with water, one dry
— check after 4–5 days with a magnifying glass (keep the bags closed, then throw
them away). Let the learner predict first.

## 3. Animal groups (`animal_groups`)

| Group | Tell-tale features (exam words) | Examples & trick members |
|---|---|---|
| Mammals | **hair or fur**; most **give birth to young alive**; **feed young with milk** | cat, human, **bat** (flies), **whale/dolphin** (live in water) |
| Birds | **feathers**, beak, two legs, wings; lay eggs with hard shells | **penguin, ostrich** (cannot fly) |
| Fish | live in water, **gills**, **fins**, most have **scales** | goldfish, shark |
| Amphibians | **moist skin** (no scales), live **on land and in water**, lay eggs in water; young (tadpole) lives in water | frog, toad, **salamander** |
| Reptiles | **dry, scaly skin**, lay eggs on land | snake, lizard, **crocodile** (lives on land and in water but is a reptile), turtle |
| Insects | see section 4 | butterfly, ant, beetle, grasshopper, mosquito |

**Traps.** Can fly ≠ bird (bat, insects). Lives in water ≠ fish (whale, dolphin,
crocodile, frog). Lays eggs ≠ bird (fish, reptiles, amphibians, insects). Salamander
looks like a lizard but has moist skin → amphibian. **Group by the feature the
question gives, not by where the animal lives.**

**Hook.** "*Fat Alligators Rarely Bite Mean Insects*" — Fish, Amphibians, Reptiles,
Birds, Mammals, Insects. Then one feature each: fins, moist, dry-scaly, feathers,
fur, 3-6-2.

**Ladder.**
- R1: "What covers a reptile's body?" (dry, scaly skin)
- R2: "Animal U has wings and feeds its young with milk. Which group?" (mammal — bat)
- R3: "Mei says a whale should be grouped with fish because it lives in water. Do
  you agree?" → CER with "gives birth to young alive / feeds young milk".

## 4. Insects (`insect_or_not`)

**Facts.** An insect has **3 body parts** (head, thorax, abdomen), **6 legs
(3 pairs)** and **1 pair of feelers**; most have wings and a hard outer covering.

**Traps.** Spider: 2 body parts, 8 legs, no feelers → not an insect. Centipede,
millipede, woodlouse: many legs → not insects. Some insects have no wings (worker
ants), so "check for wings" does **not** prove insect. To *confirm* an insect check
**legs and body parts**.

**Hook.** **3-6-2**: three parts, six legs, two feelers. Count with a pencil tip.

**Ladder.**
- R1: "How many legs does an insect have?"
- R2: Four pictures — which is an insect? (counting task)
- R3: "Give two reasons animal E (8 legs, 2 body parts) is not an insect." → two
  separate comparisons, each "E has ___ but an insect has ___".

**Try it.** Insect hunt in the garden/void deck: photograph 3 small animals, sort
insect / not insect using 3-6-2.

## 5. Classification charts and headings (`chart_heading`)

**Method (say it out loud):**
1. Read the top box — what is being sorted?
2. For a missing heading: list what **every** item under it shares **and** what the
   items on the other side **lack**. The heading must be true for all and false for
   the other branch ("Lives on land" vs "Lives in water").
3. For flowcharts: follow one diamond at a time, saying Yes/No aloud. A missing
   question must split the two items at that diamond (window pane: transparent ✓,
   metal door ✗ → "Is it transparent?").
4. Tick tables: blank cell = does **not** have it.
5. Headings come in **pairs of opposites**: can / cannot, has / does not have.

**Traps.** Headings that are true but don't separate (both groups have legs).
Writing a group name ("mammals") when a characteristic is asked — accept only if
the question allows. Forgetting the second column of a table option.

## 6. Materials (`diversity.materials` sub-skills)

**The seven materials and why we use them**

| Material | Key properties | Typical uses |
|---|---|---|
| Glass | lets most light through (transparent), waterproof, breaks easily | windows, spectacles lenses, aquarium |
| Metal | strong, waterproof, does not let light through; most sink | pots, keys, nails, drain cover, hammer head, tent pins |
| Plastic | waterproof, can be flexible, some transparent, light | raincoat, bottles, phone case window, toys |
| Rubber | flexible, waterproof | tyres, boots, hose, swimming float, eraser |
| Wood | strong, floats, does not let light through | furniture, shelves, pencils, rafts |
| Fabric | flexible, absorbent (most), not waterproof | clothes, towels, curtains (thick fabric blocks light) |
| Ceramic | waterproof, hard but breaks into pieces when dropped | cups, plates, tiles, bowls, toilets |

**Property definitions (memorise the exact words):**
- **Strength** — ability to hold heavy loads / not break easily.
- **Flexibility** — ability to **bend without breaking**. (Not "soft".)
- **Waterproof** — does **not allow water to pass through**.
- **Absorbent** — able to **soak up water**. Most absorbent = soaked up the most =
  **least water left** / **biggest increase in mass**.
- **Transparency** — how much **light passes through**. More light units on the
  sensor = more light passes through. See clearly = most light; faintly = some;
  not at all = none.
- **Float / sink** — at the top of the water = floats; at the bottom = sinks.

**Suitability = job → property → material.** First say what the object must do
(raincoat: keep body dry AND let you move), then the property each job needs
(waterproof, flexible), then pick the only material with **all** of them.
Strong is NOT always needed.

**Traps.** "Floats because it is light" — at P3 say the **material** floats. "All
metals are strong / magnetic" — keep magnetism for the magnets topic. Reading an
absorbency table the wrong way round. For a curtain to keep a room dark choose the
material letting the **least** light through; for a window/face shield/windscreen
the **most**.

**Fair tests in materials questions** (`fair_test`): changed variable = type of
material; measured = what is recorded (water left, distance bent, light units,
bricks held); kept the same = size, thickness, shape of material, amount of water,
time, distance from torch, same mass/bricks, same surface. A test is unfair if
**more than one** thing changes (e.g. different thickness).

**Ladder.**
- R1: "What does flexible mean?" (bends without breaking)
- R2: tick table → most suitable material for a raincoat / tent / food tray.
- R3: "Which material for a tent? Explain." → Claim plastic; Evidence ✓waterproof
  ✓flexible in the table; Reasoning keeps rain out, can be folded.

**Try it.** Sink-full of water + 8 objects (spoon, cork, coin, pencil, plastic
lid, eraser, key, sponge): predict float/sink, then test. Paper towel vs plastic
bag vs cloth with a teaspoon of water: which soaks it up, which lets it through?
