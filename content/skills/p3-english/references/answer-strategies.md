# How to answer: a routine for every section

Each routine has a short name so it can be called out mid-session ("Clue
Hunt!") and printed as a power-up chip on worksheets. Teach one routine at a
time, practise it on 3–5 items, then mix.

Most marks a P3 child loses are technique, not knowledge. These routines are
the fastest gains before an exam.

## 1. MCQ (grammar, vocabulary, visual text): CLUE HUNT

1. **Cover** the four options with a hand or ruler.
2. **Circle the clue**: a time word, a number word (each, every, many), who
   is doing it, near/far, a clue twin, a happy/unhappy mood.
3. **Guess** the answer before looking.
4. **Uncover** and find your guess. If it's not there, **try each option out
   loud** in the sentence.
5. **Cross out** wrong options with a reason ("these = near, but it says over
   there").

Why covering works: seeing four plausible options first makes all of them sound
right. Predicting first means the learner tests the options against their own answer.

## 2. Word-box cloze (grammar or vocabulary): READ–NAME–TICK

1. **Read** the whole passage once without filling anything in. Is the story in
   the past or present?
2. For each blank, **name** what it needs in tiny pencil: a doer pronoun, a
   past verb, an *-ly* word, a place word.
3. Choose, then **tick off** the word in the box. Each word is used once, so the
   last blanks get easier.
4. Reread each sentence with the word in.

Letter trap: write the **letter** when the instructions say letter. Some papers
skip "I" (and "O") in the letter list.

## 3. Underline-the-word cloze (SVA): FIND THE BOSS

For each bracket: put a finger over passenger phrases (of the..., in the...,
with...), ask "who or what is doing it, one or many?", then apply One S Only.
Watch for *each, every, this, there is/are, secret plurals*.

## 4. Open cloze (no helping words): WHAT KIND OF WORD?

1. Read the full sentence and the one after it.
2. Decide the word class from position (see teaching-toolkit §7, word_class).
3. Try the most common word of that kind first: *to, at, in, on, of, for,
   about, and, but, so, because, the, a, was, were, had, it, they*.
4. Only ONE word per blank. Spell it correctly.

## 5. Editing: THREE CHECKS

For each marked word, check in this order and stop at the first problem:
1. **Spelling**: sound it out in syllables; apply y→i, doubling, -ful.
2. **Tense**: is the story in the past? (*make → made*, *bring → brought*,
   *originates → originated* because "in the 19th century").
3. **Agreement**: one or many? owner pronoun right? (*Players... defend his →
   their*).
For a punctuation circle: is it the end of a thought (full stop)? a shout or
exclamation (!)? a question (?)? a pause after an opening clause (comma)?
Write the correct word **in full** in the box, spelt correctly. A right idea
with a misspelling scores zero.

## 6. Synthesis: SENTENCE SANDWICH

1. Find the joining word's job (reason, result, opposite, same time, order).
2. Write the sentence with the joining word **exactly where the lines put it**
   (start / middle / end).
3. Copy every other word exactly. Turn the second subject into a pronoun only
   if that keeps the meaning; lowercase it.
4. Starting with *Although / While / When / Before / After*? Put a comma where
   the two halves meet.
5. One capital at the start, one full stop at the end. Read it aloud.

## 7. Word order: WHO–DID–JOIN

Who → did what → join → the other part. Capital and full stop. Read aloud.
Every chunk used exactly once.

## 8. Visual text: BIG – SMALL – TRAP

1. **BIG**: read the title, the biggest words and the tagline. That is the
   main purpose and who it is for. Note who **presents** it (the organiser).
2. **SMALL**: skim each box; don't read every word yet.
3. **TRAP**: before answering, circle on the poster every **number and
   condition**: ages, prices, dates, *by*, *over*, *more than*, *only*, *early
   bird*, *free*, *per entry*, *each day*.
4. Answer each question by **pointing at** the exact words on the poster.
   No pointing, no answer.

"Which is NOT true?": test each option and mark T or F beside it. The odd one
out wins. Absolute words (*any time, all, always, never, only*) are usually
false.

## 9. Comprehension: Q-L-A (Question, Locate, Answer)

1. Read the whole passage once, like a story. Don't stop at hard words.
2. For each question: **Q**: underline the question word (who / why / how did
   ... feel / which word / what does ... refer to) and the marks [2m].
   **L**: locate the paragraph or line given, put a finger on it.
   **A**: answer in the form the question asks.

### Answer frames by question type

| Type | Frame | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Why... | "(Subject) (verb) because ..." | "He felt frustrated because he was going to miss his show." |
| How did X feel? Why? [2m] | feeling word + because + evidence | "She felt relieved (1) because she had found the hamster safe (1)." |
| Two things [2m] | (i) one idea, (ii) a different idea, each a full sentence | "(i) They comforted him. (ii) They told him he could try again next year." |
| What does 'they' refer to? | the exact noun(s) from the passage | "Grandpa and his parents" |
| Pick out a 3-word phrase | copy exactly, count on fingers | "froze in fear" |
| Which word means 'X'? | one word only, copied exactly | "dispute" |
| Circle (A) or (B) | swap test: put each into the sentence in place of the word | "rolls" for "turns" |
| True / False | find the evidence line first; check who, when, how many | "False: he took it home on Friday." |
| True/False + reason | T/F, then the evidence in your own short sentence | "False. On the first day, Riya did not want to go near the pool." |
| Sequencing | write the line number beside each event, then order by the passage (when it happened) | 2, 1, 3 |
| Lesson / character | from the last paragraph: "He learnt that..." / one character word + evidence | "He learnt to stay positive when something unexpected happens." |

### Two rules that save the most marks
* **Marks = ideas.** A 2-mark question needs two separate ideas. Count them
  before moving on.
* **Lift, then trim.** Copy the key words from the passage, then change only what
  the question needs (*I → he*, present → past). Do not add ideas from your head.

## 10. The 5-minute check (end of paper)

1. Every bracket and box filled. A guess beats a blank.
2. OAS shaded for every MCQ, matching the booklet.
3. Synthesis and word order: capital, full stop, joining word in the right place.
4. Editing: whole word written, spelt correctly.
5. Comprehension: 2-mark answers have two ideas; phrase answers have the exact
   word count.

## 11. Exam-day routine (teach it as a ritual)

* Night before: no new topics. Ten easy wins, read the power-up cards once,
  early bed.
* Morning: three warm-up items out loud (one grammar, one vocab, one "what does
  it refer to"). Wins only.
* In the hall: write **C H B T** small at the top of the rough area to recall
  the routines: **C**lue Hunt, **H**unt the boss, **B**ig–small–trap, **T**wo
  marks = two ideas.
* Stuck for more than a minute? Star it, move on, come back.
