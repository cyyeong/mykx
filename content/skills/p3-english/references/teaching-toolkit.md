# Teaching toolkit: one card per skill

Read the card for a tag before teaching it. Each card has:
**Rule** (one or two sentences a 9-year-old can say back), **Hook** (the memory
device), **Ladder** (three rungs to find exactly where it breaks), **Traps**.

How to use a ladder: ask R1. If wrong, teach at R1 and stop. If right, ask R2,
then R3. The first wrong rung is where you teach. Never teach above a broken
rung; it does not stick.

* R1 Recognise: pick the right form from two.
* R2 Apply: an exam-style item with a clue to find.
* R3 Produce or explain: write it himself, or explain why the wrong option is wrong.

## 1. Tenses and verb forms: Time Detective

**Rule.** The time clue decides the tense. Find the clue first, then pick.

| Clue words | Tense | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| every day, usually, always, often, on Sundays | simple present | walk / walks (one person: add s) |
| now, look!, listen!, keep quiet, at the moment | present continuous | is / are + -ing |
| yesterday, last week, ago, just now, earlier today, in 2020 | simple past | walked, went |
| *when* + short past event, *while* | past continuous | was / were + -ing |
| tomorrow, next time, soon, later | future | will + base |
| already, just, since, yet | present perfect | has / have + done |

**Hook: the Wave and the Zap (draw it).** Draw a long wavy line and label it
"was doing" (a long action). Draw a lightning zap hitting it: "when the lights
went out". The wave takes **was/were + -ing**; the zap takes the **simple past**.
*Mei Ling was doing (wave) her homework when the lights went out (zap).*
Drawing it is dual coding: the picture plus the words stick better than either.

**Hook: He-She-It, add an s, that's it.** Chant it with a clap on "s".

**Hook: Bossy helpers (verb_form).** *can, could, must, should, will, may, did,
does, do, has to, have to, to* are bossy: they **steal the ending** from the
next verb. *She has to practise* (not practises). *Did you finish?* (not
finished). Act it: the helper "grabs" the s off a word card.
After *before, after, without, while* (as prepositions), use **-ing**:
*before leaving, after finishing*.

**Irregular pasts: learn them in sound families** (chunking beats a long list):

| Family | Verbs |
|---|---|
| -ought / -aught | bring→brought, buy→bought, think→thought, fight→fought, catch→caught, teach→taught |
| i→a | swim→swam, sing→sang, drink→drank, begin→began, ring→rang, sit→sat, run→ran |
| -ew | fly→flew, grow→grew, know→knew, throw→threw, draw→drew, blow→blew |
| -o-e | write→wrote, ride→rode, drive→drove, wake→woke, break→broke, speak→spoke, choose→chose, freeze→froze, steal→stole |
| -t | sleep→slept, keep→kept, feel→felt, sweep→swept, leave→left, spend→spent, send→sent, build→built, lose→lost, spill→spilt |
| -ood / -ook | stand→stood, understand→understood, take→took, shake→shook |
| -old / -eld | tell→told, sell→sold, hold→held |
| no change | put, cut, hit, hurt, let, shut, set, cost, read (sounds "red") |
| one-offs | go→went, eat→ate, see→saw, come→came, give→gave, make→made, have→had, say→said, pay→paid, hear→heard, find→found, win→won, get→got, fall→fell, hide→hid |

**Ladder (tense_continuous).**
R1: "She *was reading / read* when the phone rang." (which shows she was in the middle?)
R2: "My family and I ___ at the mall when we spotted a crying child." (shopped / were shopping)
R3: "Why is *shopped* wrong?" (because shopping was the long action that was going on when the short one happened)

**Traps.** "Every student was punctual earlier today" has two clues: *every* (one)
and *earlier today* (past). A story in the past keeps every verb in the past
(editing items love *make → made* inside a past story). British spelling:
*practise* (verb), *practice* (noun); *spilt*.

## 2. Subject-verb agreement: Find the Boss

**Rule.** The verb agrees with the **boss** (the real subject), not with the
nearest noun.

**Hook: Find the Boss, ignore the passengers.** Cover the "passenger" phrase
with a finger: *Each (~~of the boys~~) brings*; *Many pails (~~of sand~~) were*;
*The box (~~of crayons~~) is*.

**Hook: One S only.** In the present tense, the s goes on the noun **or** the
verb, never both: *The dog runs. The dogs run.* Hold up one finger for "one s".

**Fixed facts (make them a 6-card set):**
1. *Each, every, one of, everyone, everybody, nobody* → singular (is, has, was, -s).
2. *This / that* standing for a whole idea → singular: *This helps.*
3. Things you cannot count are singular: *water, money, homework, information, advice, furniture, the nectar* → *is, has*.
4. *There is* + one / uncountable; *there are* + many. Look **after** the verb to find the boss.
5. Secret plurals have no s but are many: *children, people, mice, feet, teeth, police* → *are, have*.
6. *A and B* = two → plural: *Ali and his sister are...*

**Ladder.**
R1: "The cat *sleep / sleeps* on the sofa."
R2: "Each colony ___ a queen." (has / have)
R3: "Why is it *this helps* and not *this help*?"

**Traps.** "This helps squirrels stay warm": the plural noun after the verb is
not the boss. "Ants live together" vs "A single ant carries".

## 3. Pronouns: Doers, Receivers, Owners, Mirrors

**Rule.** Pick the pronoun by its **job** in the sentence.

| Job | One | Many |
|---|---|---|
| Doer (before the action) | I, you, he, she, it | we, you, they |
| Receiver (after the action or after to/for/with) | me, you, him, her, it | us, you, them |
| Owner + noun | my, your, his, her, its | our, your, their |
| Owner alone | mine, yours, his, hers | ours, yours, theirs |
| Mirror (does it to itself) | myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself | ourselves, yourselves, themselves |

**Hook: the Silly Test.** Say it out loud with the wrong one: "Him went to
the library" sounds silly → *He*. For pairs, cover the other person: *Ravi and
(I / me) went* → "me went" is silly → *I*.

**Hook: Mirror words.** Self = one, selves = many. Match the doer: us →
ourselves, students → themselves. Never *hisself* or *theirselves*.

**Hook: Owner words never take an apostrophe.** *its, hers, theirs, ours*.
*It's* only ever means *it is*.

**Cloze routine.** Before choosing, write the name the blank stands for in tiny
letters above it ("Marcus", "both boys", "the books"). Then the job decides it.

**Ladder.**
R1: "Mum gave (I / me) a sticker."
R2: Word box (he, him, his, they, them, we): "Ethan ran over to help ___."
R3: "Why not *them*?" (only Marcus needed help, so one receiver: him)

**Traps.** "I did not bring ___ today" → *mine* (owner alone, no noun after it).
"Mary and Danny... As ___ were walking" → *they* (both people). "Shall ___ take
a picture?" said by Mary to Danny → *we*.

## 4. Demonstratives and quantifiers

**Demonstratives rule and hook: the Four Hands.** Near/far × one/many.

| | One | Many |
|---|---|---|
| Near (here) | this (one finger, close) | these (spread fingers, close) |
| Far (there) | that (one finger, pointing far) | those (sweep the arm, far) |

Have the learner do the gesture for each answer. Movement keeps attention and the
gesture becomes the memory. Clue words: *here, over there, on the far table,
pointing at the next shelf, on stage, from here*.

**Quantifiers rule and hook: Can You Count It?** Put "three" in front.
"Three hours" works → countable → *many, few, a few*. "Three milks" does not →
uncountable → *much, little, a little*.

| | Countable (hours, books, friends) | Uncountable (water, money, time, information) |
|---|---|---|
| lots | many | much (questions/negatives), a lot of |
| some, positive feeling | a few | a little |
| almost none, negative feeling | few | little |

*some* = positive sentences; *any* = questions and negatives (*not, isn't, never,
hardly*). Clue for *little*: "I need to save more" (= not enough).

**Ladder.** R1: "How (many / much) apples?" R2: "There isn't ___ milk left." R3:
"Why is *a little* wrong in 'There is ___ money left. I need to save more'?"

## 5. Prepositions and connectors

**Prepositions: draw a box and a ball.** Have the learner sketch: ball *in* the box,
*on* the box, *into* the box (arrow going in), *off* the box (arrow leaving the
top), *across* the box (arrow over the top from side to side), *through* the box
(arrow in one side, out the other), *around* the box (circle arrow). Ten seconds
of drawing beats a list.

| Use | Words |
|---|---|
| Point / place | at the bus stop, at the community centre, at 7 p.m. |
| Days, surfaces | on Monday, on the table |
| Inside, months, years | in the room, in May, in 2025 |
| Movement | into (enter or hit), off (leave a surface), across (side to side), through (in and out), around, along, towards |
| Partner words (learn as pairs) | register for, wait for, look for, listen to, afraid of, proud of, full of, good at, interested in, angry with (a person), the end of, books about |

**Connectors: every joining word has a job.** Teach with a symbol each (dual
coding), and have the learner draw the symbol above the blank before choosing.

| Symbol | Job | Words |
|---|---|---|
| + | add | and |
| ↔ | opposite / surprise | but, although (never with but), however (starts a new sentence) |
| → | result | so |
| ← | reason | because, as |
| ⇉ | same time | while, when |
| 1 → 2 | order | before, after, then |

*too* goes at the end ("helped her sister too"); *also* goes in the middle
("also helped").

**Ladder.** R1: "I was hungry (so / because) I ate." R2: "Our team practised
hard, ___ we still lost." R3: "Make one sentence with *although* from: It rained.
We played."

## 6. Question tags, modals, indefinites, punctuation

**Question tags: Flip and Mirror.** Flip positive ↔ negative. Mirror the helper
and the tense. Turn the name into a pronoun.
*Sally and Jenny are friends, → aren't they?* · *These boys were in the bus, →
weren't they?* · *You have finished, → haven't you?* · No helper? Use do/does/did:
*He likes games, → doesn't he?*

**Modals by strength.** must (a rule, a firm reminder) > should (advice) >
may/can (allowed or possible) · will (future). "You ___ remember to bring a
jacket," the librarian reminded → *must*.

**Indefinites.** every-/some-/any-/no- + where/one/thing. After a negative, use
*any-*: "could not find it anywhere". "I searched everywhere but still could not
find it."

**Punctuation (editing circles).**
* A comma after an opening clause: *While they were busy, their mother...*
* Speech: *"Look at its long neck!" shouted Ben.* · *"Let's go," said Mum.* The
  punctuation sits inside the speech marks; *exclaimed, shouted, screamed* → !.
* A full stop ends every complete thought; the next word gets a capital.
* Apostrophes: *Mum's bag* (owner), *it's* = it is.

## 7. Vocabulary

### vocab.context_clues: Clue Twin + Mood Check
**Rule.** The sentence always contains a clue that means the same as the blank.
**Routine.** (1) Cover the options. (2) Underline the clue twin. (3) Mood check:
is the clue happy or unhappy, fast or slow? Cross out options with the wrong
mood. (4) Put the survivor back in and reread.
*"felt ___, like wet jelly"* → slimy. *"unsure of what was ahead"* → warily.
*"with no one able to match his speed"* → invincible.

**Movement verbs: play charades.** The learner acts one, you guess, then swap. Acting is
the memory.

| Word | Meaning | Act it |
|---|---|---|
| scampered | small quick steps, like a frightened mouse | tiny fast steps |
| staggered | walked unsteadily, about to fall (dizzy, tired, heavy load) | wobble |
| stumbled | tripped and nearly fell | catch yourself |
| trudged | slow heavy tired steps | drag your feet |
| skipped | happy little hops | skip |
| strolled | slow relaxed walk | hands in pockets |
| dashed / darted | moved very fast, suddenly | burst forward |
| tiptoed / crept | quietly | on toes |
| limped / hobbled | walking with a hurt leg | favour one leg |
| waddled | like a duck | waddle |
| wandered | walked with no plan | look around |

**Feelings ladder (weak → strong).** happy → delighted → overjoyed · sad →
upset → heartbroken · scared → nervous → terrified · angry → annoyed → furious ·
surprised → amazed → astonished. Plus: grateful (thankful), relieved (worry
gone), frustrated (blocked from what you want), embarrassed, guilty, proud,
jealous, anxious, confident, curious, lonely, disappointed, invincible
(cannot be beaten).

**Speech verbs.** wondered (asked himself), whispered, muttered (low, unclear,
often grumbling), groaned (unhappy sound), sighed, exclaimed, screamed,
explained, replied, reminded.

### vocab.similes: picture them
as wise as an owl · as fast as lightning · as quiet as a mouse · as busy as a
bee · as brave as a lion · as cold as ice · as light as a feather · as proud as
a peacock · as slow as a snail · as strong as an ox · as gentle as a lamb ·
as sly/cunning as a fox · as lonely as a cloud · as hungry as a wolf · as stubborn
as a mule · as poor as a pauper (very poor). Hook: ask the learner to draw a tiny
doodle for each. Ollie the Owl (the mascot) *is* "as wise as an owl".

### vocab.phrasal_verbs: the little word carries the meaning
out = gone or finished (put out a fire, run out of, sold out) · down = stop or
refuse (break down, turn down, calm down) · up = more or completely (speak up,
tidy up, give up, look up a word) · after = care (look after) · off = leave or
start (set off, take off) · on = continue (carry on).

### vocab.collective_nouns
herd (cows, horses, elephants) · flock (birds, sheep) · school (fish) · pack
(wolves, dogs, cards) · swarm (bees, insects) · pride (lions) · troop (monkeys) ·
bunch (bananas, grapes, flowers, keys) · team (players) · crowd (people) ·
fleet (ships) · stack (books).
Hook: "A school of fish goes to school." "A pride of lions is proud."

### vocab.confusables
* practi**c**e = noun, practi**s**e = verb (British). Hook: *ice* is a thing, so
  the noun has c (advice/advise works the same way).
* pr**e**y = the animal a pr**e**dator **e**ats; pray = talk to God.
* too = also (end of sentence) / too much; also = middle; to = direction.
* lose (lost it) / loose (not tight: "a loose goose" has two o's).
* Their (owner: has "heir") / there (place: has "here") / they're (they are).
* advice, information, knowledge, homework are uncountable: no *an*, no *s*.
* hear/here, whole/hole, piece/peace ("a piece of pie").

### vocab.word_class: what kind of word fits the gap?
After *a/the/my/some* → noun · after a verb, describing *how* → adverb (-ly):
*rushed over excitedly* · after *is/was/quite/very/so* → adjective: *quite
slippery* · after *to/will/can/nearly* → verb: *nearly stumbled*.

## 8. Spelling

**Look, Say, Cover, Write, Check** (a well-tested routine): look at the word,
say it in syllables, cover it, write it from memory, uncover and check. Three
correct in a row, then recheck tomorrow.

| Rule | Examples |
|---|---|
| consonant + y → i before -ly, -ed, -es, -er | happy→happily, hungry→hungrily, carry→carried |
| vowel + y keeps the y | enjoy→enjoyed, play→played, stay→stays |
| short vowel + one consonant → double before -ing/-ed | stop→stopped, run→running, swim→swimming |
| silent e drops before -ing | make→making, hope→hoping |
| -ful has one l | careful, beautiful, grateful |
| soft g/c before e, i, y | giraffe, giant, city, fascinated |

Words that often appear in P3 papers: vacuum, argument, different, encourage, fascinated,
reprimanded, squeaked, prey, hungrily, enjoyed, brought.
Common P3 tricky words: because, beautiful, friend (fri-END: a friend to the
END), believe, separate, necessary, surprise, tomorrow, Wednesday, February,
library, favourite, colour, neighbour (British spelling).

## 9. Synthesis and word order

**Rule.** One sentence, same meaning, joining word in the given position,
everything else copied exactly. Second-sentence pronouns lose their capital.

| Word | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| because / as | result + because + reason | Wei Jie was hungry because he had not eaten breakfast. |
| so | reason, so result | He had not eaten, so he was hungry. |
| but | idea + but + opposite | Jane went early but her friends were not there yet. |
| Although | Although A, B. (never add *but*) | Although the rain was heavy, the children played outside. |
| While | While A was -ing, B was -ing. (same time) | While Sara was reading a book, her brother was playing outside. |
| When | When short event, other action. | When the bell rang, the pupils lined up. |
| before / after + -ing | only when it is the same doer; "then" disappears | Check your work before handing it in. |
| and | same doer, two actions | Mum cooked and cleaned. |

**Hook: the Sentence Sandwich.** Bread = the two ideas, filling = the joining
word. If the filling is at the start (*Although, While, When*), there is a comma
where the sandwich is cut.

**Word order routine.** (1) Find WHO (the boss; it has a capital if it is a
name). (2) Find the action that belongs to who. (3) Find the joining word and
the other mini-sentence. (4) Capital at the start, full stop at the end. (5)
Read it aloud: if it sounds like a robot, swap a chunk.

**Ladder.** R1: "Pick: (because / but) I was tired I went to bed early." R2: a
real item with *as*. R3: an item with *Although* at the start.
