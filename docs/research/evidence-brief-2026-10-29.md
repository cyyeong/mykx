Compiled 29 Oct 2026 from web sources (WebSearch and WebFetch; PDFs read as text where possible).

**How sure each figure is**
- **[V]** I read the figure in the primary source itself (the report, the paper's full text, or the publisher's or agency's own page).
- **[A]** I read it in the paper's abstract (publisher, PubMed, arXiv or repository listing) but not the full text.
- **[2nd]** I found it only in a secondary report: news coverage, a reseller listing, or a summary. Check the primary source before putting it on a slide.

## 2. Child monitoring

### Market (all estimates are for parental-control software)

- **US$1.57 bn (2025) → US$4.12 bn (2034), 11.20% CAGR over 2026–2034** [V]
  - Fortune Business Insights, report FBI104282, updated Oct 2026. https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/parental-control-software-market-104282
- **US$1.3 bn (2021) → US$4.3 bn (2031), 13% CAGR** [2nd: the page returned 403; figures from the search snippet]
  - Allied Market Research. https://alliedmarketresearch.com/parental-control-software-market-A15427
- **US$1.5 bn (2024) → US$3.0 bn (2030), 12.1% CAGR** [2nd: via reseller]
  - Market Glass / Global Industry Analysts. https://gii.tw/report/go1644138-parental-control-software.html
  - For myKx: a niche market of a few billion dollars. Guardian is a trust feature, not the revenue story.

### How common monitoring is

- **Among US parents of 13–17-year-olds: 61% checked websites visited, 60% checked social media profiles, 48% looked through calls or messages, 39% used controls to block, filter or monitor online activity, 16% used controls to restrict phone use, and 16% tracked location with the phone** [V]
  - Pew Research Center, "Parents, Teens and Digital Monitoring", 7 Jan 2016 (fieldwork 2014–15). https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2016/01/07/parents-teens-and-digital-monitoring/
  - The data are dated, and I found no newer Pew figure on tracking teens.
- **67% of US parents of children aged 12 and under say tech companies should do more to set rules for kids online** [V]
  - Pew, 8 Oct 2025 (link above).

### What the evidence says about outcomes

- **Most of what parents know comes from what children choose to tell them, and that disclosure explained more of the link to good adjustment than tracking and surveillance did** [A]
  - The abstract says "children's spontaneous disclosure of information explained more of these relations". Parental control looked helpful only after accounting for children feeling controlled, which went with poorer adjustment.
  - Source: Kerr & Stattin, "What parents know, how they know it…", Developmental Psychology 36(3):366–380, 2000 (1,186 14-year-olds in Sweden). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10830980
  - Companion paper: Stattin & Kerr, "Parental monitoring: A reinterpretation", Child Development 71(4):1072–1085, 2000. The citation is confirmed through citing work, e.g. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2682426
  - Caveat: correlational and cross-sectional.
- **Parents who grant low autonomy were the most likely to use monitoring apps, and "increased parental control was associated with more (not fewer) online risks"** [A]
  - Ghosh, Badillo-Urquiola, Rosson, Xu, Carroll & Wisniewski, "A Matter of Control or Safety?", CHI 2018 (215 parent–teen pairs). https://doi.org/10.1145/3173574.3173768
  - Caveat: cross-sectional. Teens who had already been victimised were more likely to be monitored, so the direction of cause is unclear.
- **76% of children's app-store reviews of parental-control apps gave one star; children found the apps overly restrictive, invasive of privacy and harmful to their relationship with parents** [A]
  - Ghosh, Badillo-Urquiola, Guha, LaViola & Wisniewski, "Safety vs. Surveillance", CHI 2018 (736 reviews by 8–19-year-olds of 37 apps). https://doi.org/10.1145/3173574.3173698
- **89% of features in 75 teen-safety apps supported parental control (monitoring and restriction); 11% supported teens' self-regulation** [2nd: summaries disagree on whether the 89% counts features or apps]
  - Wisniewski et al., "Parental Control vs. Teen Self-Regulation", CSCW 2017, pp. 51–69. https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/parental-control-vs-teen-self-regulation-is-there-a-middle-ground/
- **Restrictive mediation cut children's media time more, but active mediation (talking about media) and co-use did more to reduce media-related risks** [A]
  - Chen & Shi, "Reducing Harm From Media: A Meta-Analysis of Parental Mediation", Journalism & Mass Communication Quarterly 96(1):173–193, 2019 (52 studies, N=74,159). https://doi.org/10.1177/1077699018754908
- **Parents' support for autonomy goes with higher achievement and better psychosocial functioning; the strongest link is with psychological health** [A]
  - Vasquez, Patall, Fong, Corrigan & Pine, Educational Psychology Review 28(3):605–644, 2016 (36 studies). https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/2016_VasquezPatallFongCorriganPine_EPR.pdf

**Design choices this evidence supports**
1. Make monitoring visible to the child (the chip). Knowledge built on disclosure, not hidden surveillance, is what goes with good outcomes.
2. Give older children agency, such as the teen's 30-minute pause with the parent told. Low-autonomy, restrictive use is where the risks cluster.
3. Use alerts as prompts for a conversation (Right / Not right review, "possibly" wording), not for automatic restriction. Active mediation beats restriction on risk.
4. Offer the child self-regulation tools, not only parent controls. That is the gap Wisniewski et al. found.

**Limits:** almost all of this evidence is correlational. I found no trial showing that transparent monitoring improves safety, and no research on outcomes for AI-read home cameras watching children. Say "designed in line with research on trust and disclosure", not "proven safer".

## 3. Learning science behind myKx

### 3a. One-to-one tutoring and adaptive tutors

- **Bloom's "2 sigma" claim rests on two small dissertations: 3-week units, narrow tests, and tutoring bundled with mastery testing. A realistic effect for good tutoring is "About one-third of a standard deviation"** [V]
  - Bloom, Educational Researcher 13(6):4–16, 1984, as critiqued by von Hippel, "Two-Sigma Tutoring: Separating Science Fiction from Science Fact", Education Next 24(2), Spring 2024. https://www.educationnext.org/two-sigma-tutoring-separating-science-fiction-from-science-fact/
- **Pooled effect of tutoring in pre-K–12 randomised trials: 0.37 SD** [A]
  - Effects are larger for teacher and paraprofessional tutors, in the early grades, and during the school day.
  - Nickow, Oreopoulos & Quan, NBER Working Paper 27476, Jul 2020. https://www.nber.org/papers/w27476
- **Against no tutoring: human tutoring d=0.79, step-based intelligent tutoring systems (ITS) d=0.76** [2nd: abstract figures as reported by several secondary sources; the publisher page returned 403]
  - VanLehn, Educational Psychologist 46(4):197–221, 2011. https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2011.611369
- **Median ITS effect of 0.66 SD across 50 controlled evaluations; effects were much smaller on standardised tests than on locally built tests** [A]
  - Kulik & Fletcher, Review of Educational Research 86(1):42–78, 2016. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315581420
- **ITS against other teaching (107 effect sizes, 14,321 participants)** [V]
  - Against teacher-led large-group teaching g=.42; against other computer-based instruction .57; against textbooks .35.
  - No significant difference from one-to-one human tutoring (−.11) or small groups (.05).
  - Source: Ma, Adesope, Nesbit & Liu, Journal of Educational Psychology 106(4):901–918, 2014. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037123
  - For myKx: this is the core "a good tutor for every child" evidence. Step-level hints and feedback are the active ingredient.
- **Tech-supported personalised learning in low- and middle-income countries: overall effect 0.18; approaches that adapt to the learner's level 0.35, which beat other approaches** [A]
  - Major, Francis & Tsapali, British Journal of Educational Technology, 2021 (16 RCTs, 53,029 learners aged 6–15). https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/176999
  - For myKx: adapting to the child's level matters more than personalising the surface.

### 3b. AI-tutor trials, 2024–2025

- **Harvard physics: students learned more than twice as much with an AI tutor as in an in-class active-learning lesson; effect 0.73–1.3 SD by quantile regression (0.63 by ordinary regression); median 49 minutes on task** [V]
  - Kestin, Miller, Klales, Milbourne & Ponti, Scientific Reports 15:17458, 2025 (194 students, crossover design). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/
  - Limits: one lesson per condition, undergraduates, no test of retention.
- **Nigeria: about 0.3 SD after a 6-week after-school English programme using a GPT-4 tutor; it outperformed 80% of interventions in a database of RCTs in developing countries** [V on the World Bank authors' blog]
  - De Simone et al., World Bank Policy Research Working Paper 11125, 2025. https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/education/From-chalkboards-to-chatbots-Transforming-learning-in-Nigeria
- **Ghana: effect 0.37 on maths growth for a WhatsApp maths tutor ("Rori") used 2 × 30 minutes a week for 8 months** [A, preprint]
  - About 1,000 students in grades 3–9.
  - Henkel et al., arXiv 2402.09809, 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09809
- **Students whose human tutors had AI suggestions in real time were 4 percentage points more likely to master topics, and 9 points more when the tutor was lower-rated; cost about US$20 per tutor per year** [A]
  - 900 tutors and 1,800 students.
  - Wang, Ribeiro, Robinson, Loeb & Demszky, "Tutor CoPilot", arXiv 2410.03017. https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03017
  - For myKx: well-designed AI tutoring shows real but moderate gains. Every positive trial built teaching structure into the tutor.

### 3c. Mastery learning and adaptive pacing

- **Mastery learning programmes improved exam performance in 108 controlled evaluations; effects were stronger for weaker students** [A]
  - The same abstract says the programmes may take more time and that self-paced versions often cut completion rates in college classes.
  - Kulik, Kulik & Bangert-Drowns, Review of Educational Research 60(2):265–299, 1990. https://www.academia.edu/81783373/Effectiveness_of_Mastery_Learning_Programs_A_Meta_Analysis
  - The "0.52" figure often quoted for this paper is unverified; don't use it.
- **Group-based mastery learning in K-12 over 4 weeks or more: "essentially no evidence" of effects on standardised tests; moderate effects on experimenter-made tests, with little evidence they last** [V]
  - Slavin, "Mastery Learning Reconsidered", Review of Educational Research 57(2):175–213, 1987. https://www.gwern.net/doc/psychology/1987-slavin.pdf
- **EEF Toolkit, mastery learning: +5 months on average, but resting on limited evidence** [2nd: EEF pages as shown in search; the site blocked automated fetches]
  - Results are wide-spread. The strand is reportedly less effective when pupils work at their own pace, and works best with a high mastery bar (80–90%).
  - https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/mastery-learning
  - For myKx: deterministic mastery gates fit the evidence. Pure self-pacing is a known weak spot, so pair it with Kix's nudges and the parent summary, and don't promise standardised-test gains.

### 3d. Retrieval practice and spacing

- **One week after studying: 56% recalled after testing vs 42% after restudying (d=0.83); 61% after three tests vs 40% after four study sessions. Restudying won at 5 minutes (81% vs 75%)** [V]
  - Roediger & Karpicke, "Test-Enhanced Learning", Psychological Science 17(3):249–255, 2006. https://psychology.ecu.edu/wp-content/pv-uploads/sites/216/2019/03/Roediger-Karpicke-2006.pdf
  - Caveat: university students learning prose passages.
- **Of 10 study techniques, only practice testing and distributed practice were rated "high utility"; rereading and highlighting were rated low** [A]
  - Dunlosky, Rawson, Marsh, Nathan & Willingham, Psychological Science in the Public Interest 14(1):4–58, 2013. https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266
- **Practice tests beat restudying and every other comparison condition (272 effect sizes, 15,472 participants)** [A]
  - Adesope, Trevisan & Sundararajan, Review of Educational Research 87(3):659–701, 2017. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316689306
- **EEF (2021, 295 school-based studies): cognitive-science approaches can have a meaningful impact, but evidence on applying them in classrooms is limited, and thinnest for younger children and primary schools** [2nd]
  - https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/evidence-reviews/cognitive-science-approaches-in-the-classroom
  - For myKx: low-stakes quizzes and spaced review are well supported in the lab. For P3 children, claim "research-based", not "proven in classrooms".

### 3e. Feedback and metacognition (EEF Toolkit; England-based; "months" are average extra progress over a year)

- **Feedback: +6 months; high impact, very low cost, extensive evidence. Primary +7, secondary +5, digital feedback about +4** [2nd: EEF page text via search]
  - https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/feedback
- **Metacognition and self-regulation: +8 months, raised from +7 in the 21 May 2025 update, which added 107 studies** [2nd: EEF news via search]
  - The strand notes that digital tools such as ITS that scaffold learning show particularly high impacts.
  - https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/news/updates-ten-strands-toolkit
- **One-to-one tuition: +5 months; best as short, regular sessions (about 30 minutes, 3–5 times a week, for up to 10 weeks)** [2nd]
  - https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/one-to-one-tuition
- **Feedback raised performance by d=.41 on average, but over a third of feedback interventions lowered it; effects shrink when feedback draws attention to the self rather than the task** [A]
  - Kluger & DeNisi, Psychological Bulletin 119(2):254–284, 1996 (607 effect sizes). Abstract text came back in search results from the first author's university pages (https://scholars.huji.ac.il/node/1802); I did not open the paper.
- **Feedback: d=0.48 across 435 studies, with high variation; how much information the feedback carries matters most** [A]
  - Wisniewski, Zierer & Hattie, Frontiers in Psychology 10:3087, 2020. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03087
  - For myKx: Kix's hints should be about the task ("check the units in step 2"), not praise of the person. "Explain how you got it" prompts are metacognition.

### 3f. Teaching through the child's interests

- **Algebra problems rewritten around students' own interests improved their skill at writing algebraic expressions, and the gain held in a later unit with no personalisation; struggling students gained most** [2nd: summaries; I could not check effect sizes]
  - Walkington, Journal of Educational Psychology 105(4):932–945, 2013 (145 students using Cognitive Tutor). https://edsurge.com/news/2013-11-08-can-interest-based-personalization-deliver-better-learning-outcomes
- **Personalisation raised triggered situational interest, which predicted accuracy and efficiency in the tutor; there was a total effect on classroom exam scores and on lasting interest in maths** [A]
  - Bernacki & Walkington, Journal of Educational Psychology 110(6):864–881, 2018 (about 150 students). https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000250
- **Interest develops in four phases, from triggered situational interest to well-developed individual interest** [A]
  - Hidi & Renninger, Educational Psychologist 41(2):111–127, 2006. https://doi.org/10.1207/s15326985ep4102_4
  - Caveat: a later study describes the model as having "limited empirical support".
- **Earlier experimental source for contextualisation, personalisation and choice** [citation confirmed; findings not re-checked]
  - Cordova & Lepper, Journal of Educational Psychology 88(4):715–730, 1996. https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/1996/intrinsic-motivation-and-the-process-of-learning-beneficial-effects/
  - For myKx: framing problems in the child's own interests (dinosaurs, idols) is well grounded. It works when the interest shapes the problem itself; just swapping in a theme name lifts interest more than learning.

### 3g. Games, gamification and rewards

- **Digital games against non-game teaching: g=0.33 (k=57); enhanced game designs against basic ones: g=0.34** [A]
  - Clark, Tanner-Smith & Killingsworth, Review of Educational Research 86(1):79–122, 2016. https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654315582065
- **Serious games: learning d=0.29, retention d=0.36, but not more motivating than conventional teaching (d=0.26, not significant)** [A]
  - Games did better when combined with other teaching, used over several sessions, or played in groups.
  - Wouters, van Nimwegen, van Oostendorp & van der Spek, Journal of Educational Psychology 105(2):249–265, 2013. https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031311
- **Gamification: cognitive g=0.49, motivational g=0.36, behavioural g=0.25; only the cognitive effect held up in the most rigorous studies** [A]
  - Sailer & Homner, Educational Psychology Review 32(1):77–112, 2020. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w
- **A course with a leaderboard and badges led over a semester to lower intrinsic motivation, satisfaction and final-exam scores** [2nd]
  - Hanus & Fox, Computers & Education 80:152–161, 2015. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2014.08.019
- **Rewards and intrinsic motivation (128 studies)** [V]
  - Expected, contingent tangible rewards undermined free-choice intrinsic motivation: all tangible rewards d=−0.34 (92 studies); performance-contingent d=−0.28.
  - Unexpected tangible rewards had no effect (d=0.01). Positive feedback raised free-choice behaviour (d=0.33) and interest (d=0.31).
  - Tangible rewards hurt children more than college students.
  - Deci, Koestner & Ryan, Psychological Bulletin 125(6):627–668, 1999. https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf
  - For myKx: no leaderboards (already a hard rule) is supported. The Treasure Realm should feel like informative celebration of mastery, with surprise moments, not pay per task. Don't claim games or rewards raise motivation.

### 3h. Visuals and multimedia

- **Multimedia principle: words plus relevant pictures beat words alone in 11 of 11 transfer tests; median d=1.39; stronger for low-knowledge learners** [V]
  - Mayer, Multimedia Learning (2nd ed.), ch. 12, Cambridge University Press, 2009. https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/multimedia-learning/multimedia-principle/1CC3DE892B0431BA48B4C4DCA10D0B8F
  - Caveat: these are Mayer's own lab studies, mostly short lessons. Dual coding theory gives the rationale: Clark & Paivio, Educational Psychology Review 3(3), 1991. https://clark.faculty.uwinnipeg.ca/Clark/Publications/1991-Clark&Paivio-DCT&Educ.pdf
- **Seductive details (interesting but irrelevant content) lowered learning: g=−0.33 across 68 effects in 58 studies with over 7,500 learners** [2nd for g; press release for the rest]
  - The harm was worse when the detail sat beside a relevant diagram or stayed on screen. Most studies lasted only 6–12 minutes.
  - Sundararajan & Adesope, Educational Psychology Review 32(3):707–734, 2020. https://news.wsu.edu/press-release/2020/03/19/seductive-details-inhibit-learning
  - An earlier meta-analysis (Rey, Educational Research Review 7(3), 2012; 39 effects) also found negative effects. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.edurev.2012.05.003
- **Kindergarteners in a heavily decorated room were off task more (39% vs 28%) and gained less (18% vs 33%)** [2nd for the numbers]
  - Fisher, Godwin & Seltman, Psychological Science 25(7):1362–1370, 2014 (n=24). Numbers from https://fordhaminstitute.org/ohio/commentary/visual-environment-attention-allocation-and-learning
  - Critique: novelty may explain the effect, since distraction fell across lessons. Imuta & Scarf, Frontiers in Psychology 5:1444, 2014. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4274873/
  - For myKx: visual richness is a strength only when the art carries the concept. Keep the question player calm and save ambient spectacle for Home and Treasure. This is a real tension with "visually stunning".

### 3i. Attention, breaks and emotion

- **10-minute classroom activity breaks raised on-task behaviour by 8% (ES 0.60) and by 20% among the least on-task pupils (ES 2.20)** [A]
  - Mahar et al., Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise 38(12):2086–2094, 2006 (on-task observation of 62 grade 3–4 pupils). https://doi.org/10.1249/01.mss.0000235359.16685.a3
- **Meta-analysis of classroom physical activity: on-task behaviour SMD 0.60 (95% CI 0.20–1.00); cognitive effect 0.33, not significant; high heterogeneity** [A]
  - Watson et al., International Journal of Behavioral Nutrition and Physical Activity, 2017 (16 studies meta-analysed). https://doi.org/10.1186/s12966-017-0569-9
  - EEF rates physical activity at +1 month for attainment [2nd]. Breaks help attention, not test scores directly.
- **Correlations with achievement across 68 studies: enjoyment ρ=.27, boredom ρ=−.25, anger ρ=−.35, frustration ρ=−.02** [A]
  - Camacho-Morles, Slemp, Pekrun et al., Educational Psychology Review 33(3):1051–1095, 2021. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09585-3
  - Caveat: correlational.
- **Boredom was very persistent and went with poorer learning and gaming the system; frustration was less persistent and did not lead to gaming. The authors recommend detecting and responding to boredom** [A]
  - Baker, D'Mello, Rodrigo & Graesser, "Better to be frustrated than bored", International Journal of Human-Computer Studies 68(4):223–241, 2010. https://archium.ateneo.edu/discs-faculty-pubs/92
- **Stress impairs retrieval (exam-type recall), and its effects on learning depend on timing** [A, review]
  - Vogel & Schwabe, npj Science of Learning, 2016. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6380371/
  - For myKx: a gentle start after a hard day, short movement breaks, and a curiosity spark when boredom shows are all consistent with this evidence. None of it is tested as a package.

### 3j. Risks and balance

- **High-school maths with GPT-4: practice scores rose 48% with plain GPT-4 and 127% with a hint-giving tutor version. When access was removed, the plain-GPT-4 group scored 17% below a control group that never had access; the tutor's guardrails largely removed this harm** [A]
  - Bastani, Bastani, Sungu, Ge, Kabakcı & Mariman, "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning", PNAS 122(26), 2025 (about 1,000 students in Turkey). https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122
  - For myKx: the strongest support for Kix giving hints, never answers.
- **Screen-time guidance**
  - WHO (24 Apr 2019): no sedentary screen time for 1-year-olds; for ages 2–4, "no more than 1 hour; less is better" [V]. https://www.who.int/news/item/24-04-2019-to-grow-up-healthy-children-need-to-sit-less-and-play-more
  - Singapore MOH, Grow Well SG (21 Jan 2025) [V]. https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/grow-well-sg-to-support-families--in-building-healthy-habits-in-children-/
    - Ages 3–6: under 1 hour a day of screen use outside school.
    - Ages 7–12: under 2 hours a day "unless related to schoolwork".
    - No unrestricted access to mobile devices, and no social media.
  - American Academy of Pediatrics (2016, Pediatrics 138(5):e20162592): the evidence does not support one-size-fits-all limits; recommends a Family Media Use Plan [2nd]. https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2017/0701/p56.html
  - For myKx: show learning time against MOH's guidance in the parent summary. Some of myKx's play surfaces (Vibe Zone, Arcade) count as recreational screen time and should be shown as such.
- **PISA 2022: students using devices for leisure up to 1 hour a day scored 49 maths points more than those using them 5–7 hours (after adjusting for socio-economic status); being distracted by others' devices went with 15 points lower** [2nd: OECD release text via search; correlational]
  - OECD, Dec 2023. https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2023/12/decline-in-educational-performance-only-partly-attributable-to-the-covid-19-pandemic.html
- **Evidence on edtech's impact is mixed and its costs are underestimated; technology should be adopted on evidence that it is appropriate, equitable, scalable and sustainable** [2nd]
  - UNESCO, GEM Report 2023, "Technology in education: A tool on whose terms?". https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000385723
- **For parents: academic socialisation (linking school to the child's goals) was the most effective kind of involvement; direct homework help was not, and some reports describe a negative association** [2nd]
  - Hill & Tyson, Developmental Psychology 45(3):740–763, 2009. https://www.gse.harvard.edu/ideas/usable-knowledge/09/10/parental-involvement-puzzle
  - For myKx: the evening summary should give the parent one conversation to have, not a checklist of homework to police.

## Five strongest figures for the impact pitch

1. **56.6%** of Singapore married couples are dual-career (2025, up from 52.5% in 2020). DOS General Household Survey 2025 [V].
2. **S$1.8 bn** spent on private tuition in Singapore in 2023, with top-quintile households spending S$162.60 a month against S$36.30 for the bottom quintile. DOS HES 2023 via The Straits Times [2nd, reliable].
3. **Intelligent tutors beat large-group teaching (g=.42) and are not significantly different from one-to-one human tutoring** (107 effect sizes). Ma et al. 2014 [V].
4. **Unguarded GPT-4 left students 17% worse once it was taken away; a hint-only tutor largely avoided this** (about 1,000 students). Bastani et al., PNAS 2025 [A]. This is the case for how Kix is designed.
5. **Restrictive monitoring backfires:** "increased parental control was associated with more (not fewer) online risks" (Ghosh et al., CHI 2018 [A]), and child disclosure, not surveillance, explains good outcomes (Kerr & Stattin 2000 [A]). This is the case for the child-visible Guardian.

Alternates: tutoring averages 0.37 SD (Nickow et al. 2020); a 6-week AI tutor in Nigeria gave about 0.3 SD (World Bank 2025); Singapore parents spend 7.9 h a week helping with schoolwork against a 6.7 h global average (Varkey 2018).

## Claims NOT to make (the evidence is weak, contested or misread)

- **"AI tutoring delivers Bloom's 2-sigma."** The 2-sigma figure is inflated; realistic tutoring effects are about 0.3–0.4 SD.
- **"AI tutors double learning."** That comes from one Harvard undergraduate lesson (Kestin 2025) and does not generalise to P3–Sec 2 or to retention.
- **Specific standardised-test gains from mastery learning or ITS.** Effects shrink sharply on standardised tests (Slavin 1987; Kulik & Fletcher 2016).
- **"Games and rewards boost motivation."** Games were not more motivating (Wouters 2013), gamification's motivational effects are unstable (Sailer & Homner 2020), and expected tangible rewards undermine intrinsic motivation (Deci et al. 1999).
- **"Rich visuals improve learning."** Only relevant visuals do. Decorative and seductive content can hurt (Sundararajan & Adesope 2020; Fisher et al. 2014).
- **"myKx adapts to visual learners."** Nothing reviewed here supports matching teaching to learning styles; the multimedia principle applies to all learners.
- **Any "attention span = age × N minutes" rule.** I found no robust source.
- **"Detecting a child's mood improves learning."** The emotion–achievement links are correlational, and I found no trial of mood-adaptive tutoring for children.
- **"Monitoring keeps children safe," or "transparent monitoring is proven safer."** The evidence is correlational, and there is none for AI-read home cameras.
- **A single "AI in education market size".** Firms differ about tenfold (MarketsandMarkets US$5.82 bn by 2030 against Grand View US$57.2 bn by 2033).
- **"China's 2021 rules ended tutoring."** This is contested (Liu et al. 2026).
- **"Most Singapore children have tuition."** No official share was found; the S$104.80 is an average over all households.
- **Exact figures I could not verify:** Kulik et al. 1990 "ES 0.52"; Adesope 2017 "g=0.61"; an EEF figure for "digital technology"; Japan's MEXT tutoring spend (corrected Mar 2026); any recent official Hong Kong or US tuition-spend figure; a like-for-like OECD dual-earner average.
- **Claims from small, non-peer-reviewed studies about AI chatbots and "brain" or "cognitive decline".** I did not review them; leave them out.
